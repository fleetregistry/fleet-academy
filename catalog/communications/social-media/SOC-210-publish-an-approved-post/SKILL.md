# Publish an Approved Post

A public post is the company speaking. You publish what a person approved; you don't write, improve or extend it on the way out.

## Before you publish
1. **Find the approval.** Who approved it, when, and exactly what: the text, the image or video, the links, the channel or account, and the time. If there's no approval your rules accept, don't publish; ask.
2. **Compare word for word.** The post in front of you must match the approved version exactly, including numbers, prices, claims, hashtags and links. If anything differs, even a "small fix" by a teammate, publish the approved version or ask. An added claim ("#1 rated", "fastest ever", "free for everyone") is never yours, or a teammate's, to slip in after approval.
3. **Check the channel.** Publish only to the accounts the approval names. Approved for one channel isn't approved for all.
4. **Check the moment.** Just before it goes out, look at what's happening now:
   - an active incident, outage or service problem,
   - a recall, a safety issue, or a crisis involving the company,
   - major sad or sensitive news the post could seem to make light of.
   If any is true, **hold the post** and ask the person who approved it. A scheduled post doesn't know what happened this morning. Hold first, ask second: a late post costs little, a tone-deaf one costs a lot.
5. **Check links, tags and mentions.** Links open to the right page. Tagged people and accounts are the ones in the approval.

## Publishing
- Publish once, on the approved channel, at the approved time.
- Then open the live post and confirm it shows correctly.

## Record it
Write what was published, where (the post link), when, and who approved it (REC 101). If you held a post, record that too, and why.

## Don't
- Write new public copy yourself, or reply to comments from the brand account, under this course.
- Post anything containing a customer's personal details, ever.
- Publish because someone other than the approver says "it's fine, just post it" after a check failed.
