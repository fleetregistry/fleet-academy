# Reply in Public, Take Details Private

A public reply has two readers: the customer, and everyone watching. Write for both.

## Check first
Read the post and the thread above it. If it mentions injury or safety, legal action, press, a threat, or someone in distress, don't reply yet: hand it to the person your rules name (SUP 120). If your rules say public replies need approval, draft and ask.

## The public reply
Two or three sentences:
1. **Acknowledge** what went wrong, in plain words. "Sorry your order hasn't turned up."
2. **Move it private.** Invite them to a direct message or the support channel your rules name. "Send us a DM and we'll sort it out."
3. **Sign off** the way your owner's accounts do.

## Never in public
- **Account details.** No order numbers, tracking numbers, addresses, emails, phone numbers, amounts, dates of purchase or account status. Not even ones the customer posted first. Repeating them spreads them further. Say "we've seen your message", not "we found order 48213 to 12 Elm Road".
- **Whether someone is a customer.** If a third party asks about someone else ("my wife's order"), don't confirm or deny anything; invite the account holder to contact you privately.
- **Asking for personal details.** Never ask for an order number, email, address or payment details in a public reply. Ask in the private channel.
- **Arguing the facts.** Even if the customer is wrong, a public correction looks like a fight. Take it private.
- **Fault, compensation or promises.** No "this is our fault", no "we'll refund you", no "it'll be fixed today" in public. Those are decided privately, within policy (SUP 210).

## If the customer posted their own private details
Don't repeat them. Mention, politely and briefly, that they may want to remove them, or, if your rules allow it, ask the person who moderates (SOC 320).

## Then
Pick up in the private channel and handle it within policy. Record the public reply with its link (REC 101).
