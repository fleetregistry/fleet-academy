# Before You Edit or Delete a Public Post

A published post is part of the record. Every check below must pass before you change or remove one. If any fails, change nothing and ask.

## 1. The right person approved this exact change
- Your rules say who may approve changes to published posts. Only that person's approval counts, not a teammate's, not the person who wrote the post unless the rules name them.
- The approval covers **this** post (link), and **this** change: what it says now, and what it will say after, or that it will be removed.
- If the rules don't say who approves, treat it as "ask first": ask your owner.

## 2. A copy of the original is kept
Before any change, save the post's full text, images, link, time posted, and its likes, shares and replies at that moment, where your owner keeps records. A change you can't show the "before" of can't be explained later.

## 3. A visible correction beats a silent one
- If nobody has engaged yet (a typo spotted within minutes), a quiet edit is usually fine.
- Once people have replied, shared or quoted it, or the press has picked it up, **say what changed**: an edit with a visible correction note, or a reply or new post that corrects it. A silent rewrite or deletion makes the replies look wrong and the company look like it's hiding something.
- Deleting is the last choice, for things that must not stay up at all (a customer's private data, something unsafe or unlawful), and still needs the approval in check 1.

## 4. Nothing under a hold
Never edit or delete a post, or comments on it, that is tied to a legal hold, a complaint to a regulator, a dispute, or an active news story, unless the person your rules name for legal matters says so. If you don't know, ask.

## 5. One post at a time
No bulk deletions ("everything mentioning the recall", "all posts before March"). List each post with its link and the reason, and get each approved. If you're asked to delete in bulk, say no and offer the list.

## 6. Record it
For each change: the post link, what it said before, what it says now (or "removed"), when, who approved it, and where the saved copy is (REC 101).

## Never
- Delete or hide a post to make criticism or a mistake disappear.
- Edit a post so that replies to it no longer make sense, without a visible note.
