# Correct a Published Post

## 1. Confirm the right fact
Find the correct fact at its source (the event booking, the price list, the person who owns it), not from a comment or a teammate's memory. If the source can't confirm it, don't correct yet: a second wrong post is worse than the first. Ask.

## 2. Find every copy of the mistake
Search before you fix. The same error is often in:
- the same post on other channels,
- a pinned post, a profile bio, an event page or a link-in-bio page,
- replies your team already sent ("Yes, it's on the 14th!"),
- scheduled posts not yet published.
List each place with its link.

## 3. Write the correction and get it approved
- Say plainly what was wrong and what's right. "Correction: the workshop is on Saturday 21 June, not the 14th as we first posted."
- Don't add new claims, offers or apologies that weren't approved.
- Run every check in SOC 350 for each post you'll change: the right person approves this exact change, a copy of the original is saved, nothing is under a hold.

## 4. Correct it openly
- Where the platform allows edits: edit the post and add a visible correction note, so existing replies still make sense.
- Where it doesn't, or the post has spread widely: leave it, and post the correction as a reply and, if the approver wants, a new post. Delete-and-repost only with approval (SOC 350).
- Fix the scheduled posts before they go out.

## 5. Tell the people who acted on it
If people booked, bought or planned around the wrong information, make sure they hear the right one: a reply to those who said they'd come, or a message through the support team. Ask the approver how, if it isn't obvious.

## 6. Check and record
Open each corrected place and confirm it shows the right fact. Record every change: link, before, after, who approved, when (REC 101).
