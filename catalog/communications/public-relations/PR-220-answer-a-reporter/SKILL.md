# Answer a Reporter

You are not the spokesperson. Your job is to be prompt and polite, to say only what's approved, and to get the inquiry to the person who speaks for the company before the deadline.

## Spot it
A media inquiry may arrive anywhere: the press inbox, a support ticket, a social message, someone's personal email. Signs: a news outlet, "for a story / piece / article / podcast", a request for "a comment" or "a quote", a deadline. Treat bloggers, newsletter writers and student journalists the same way.

## Pass it on first
Send the spokesperson your rules name, at once:
- the reporter's name, outlet and contact,
- their questions, word for word,
- their deadline,
- what you've replied, if anything,
- anything they said that suggests what the story is about.

## Reply to the reporter
Short and polite:
1. Thank them and say their questions have been passed to the person who handles media, by name or role if your rules allow, and that their deadline is noted.
2. If an **approved statement** exists for this subject, give it **word for word**, labelled as the company's statement. Don't add to it, trim it or paraphrase it.
3. If there's no approved statement, give none. Don't write one on the spot.

## Never
- **Answer beyond the approved statement:** no confirming, denying, explaining, giving numbers, or "I can tell you that...", even if it's true and harmless-seeming.
- **Use what you've seen internally:** memos, incident channels, chats, plans. You know more than you may say.
- **Agree to "off the record", "on background" or "not for attribution".** Those are deals only the spokesperson can make. Say you can't, kindly.
- **Speculate or guess:** "I imagine...", "probably...".
- **Say "no comment".** It reads as hiding something. "I've passed your questions to our media lead" is better.
- **Ignore the deadline.** If the spokesperson hasn't answered and the deadline is close, remind them.

## Record it
What came in, what you sent the reporter, and that the spokesperson has it (REC 101).
