# Draft a Statement From Confirmed Facts

## Start from the fact sheet
Use a fact sheet (PR 110). Every factual sentence in the statement must trace to a **confirmed** item. If there's no fact sheet, build one first.

## The shape
Short. Usually under 150 words.
1. **What happened,** in plain words, from confirmed facts only: what, who was affected, when.
2. **What we're doing,** only actions that are really under way.
3. **What's still being looked into,** said plainly: "We're still investigating the cause." Honest uncertainty is better than a confident claim you may have to take back.
4. **What people should do,** if anything, and where to get help.
5. **When we'll update,** only if the owner or spokesperson set a time.

## Words that overreach
Leave these out unless the fact sheet confirms them, word for word:
- **"No data was affected / accessed / lost."** "We have seen no evidence of X so far" is a different claim; use it only if that's exactly what was confirmed, and keep "so far".
- **"Fully resolved", "fixed", "back to normal"** while it's still being watched or only partly fixed. Say what's true: "A fix is in place and we're monitoring."
- **"Isolated", "small number", "only a few"** without a confirmed count.
- **A cause** still under investigation, and **blame** of a vendor, partner, employee or customer.
- **Exact figures** that aren't confirmed.

## Promises need a yes
A refund, credit, compensation, deadline, or "this will never happen again" is a promise. Include one only if a person with authority approved that exact promise. If someone asks for it but it isn't approved, leave it out and say so in your note.

## Never in a statement
Names of customers or staff, anything Legal said not to discuss, security details that would help an attacker.

## Hand it over
- Send the draft to the spokesperson or approver your rules name, marked as a draft. Never publish it yourself under this course.
- Beside the draft, list any requested wording you left out and why ("'No data affected' isn't confirmed; security has only seen no access so far").
