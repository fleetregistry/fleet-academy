# Say No in Writing

## Before you write, get four things straight
1. **The decision.** Is it no, or not now? If it's "not now", is there a real time ("we'll review sponsorships in January")? If you can't tell, ask the owner. Don't guess toward the kinder one.
2. **The reason you may give.** It must be true. Use the owner's reason, unless it's confidential or unkind. Then give a true, general one ("it isn't a fit for our plans this year"), never an invented one ("our budget is fully spent" when it isn't).
3. **Any alternative.** Only one the owner has approved, or one that already exists publicly (a published application form, a public programme). Never invent one to soften the letter.
4. **How open the door is.** "Please do get in touch next year" only if the owner would welcome that.

## The shape
1. **Thanks, briefly.** One line.
2. **The decision, in the first two sentences.** "We've decided not to sponsor the festival this year." Not "unfortunately at this time we are unable to proceed with...". Plain words.
3. **The reason,** in one or two sentences.
4. **The alternative,** if there is an approved one.
5. **A courteous close** that matches the door.

## Keep out
- **Confidential reasons:** plans, finances, other deals, people matters, anything the owner said privately. "We're about to be acquired" stays out, even if it's the true reason.
- **False hope:** "maybe next time", "we'll keep you in mind", "who knows", unless the owner means it.
- **Blame:** of colleagues, the requester, or other parties.
- **Admissions:** don't apologize for or admit a fault that hasn't been established. Be sorry to disappoint, not sorry for wrongdoing.
- **Hedges that undo the no:** "unless you could...", "if anything changes...", when nothing will.

## Before sending
Read only the first two sentences. Would the reader know the answer? If not, rewrite. Then send as your rules allow (BOPS 210), and record it (REC 101).
