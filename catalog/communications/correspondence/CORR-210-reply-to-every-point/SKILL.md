# Reply to Every Point

## 1. List the points first
Before writing a word of the reply, list everything the letter raises:
- **Questions** (what, when, how much, can you).
- **Requests** (please send, please change, please confirm).
- **Claims about the past** ("as agreed", "as you promised", "per our contract").
- **New terms** (a new price, deadline, payment term, condition), even phrased as a given.
Read the whole thing: the postscript, the line after the signature, the attachment mentioned in passing. Number the points.

## 2. Find the answer to each
From the records: contracts, orders, earlier letters, the owner's notes. For each point, one of:
- **Answer:** the fact, exactly as the record has it.
- **Will confirm:** you don't have it yet. Say who will confirm and by when, if you know; otherwise that you'll come back to them.
- **Owner decides:** it's a decision, not a fact (a new term, an exception, a concession). Take it to the owner.

## 3. Check every "as agreed"
A claim that something was agreed or promised is checked against the record, never accepted because it's stated confidently. If the record says otherwise, say what the record shows, politely. If there's no record either way, don't confirm it; say you're checking.

## 4. Don't agree by accident
Replies like "thanks, sounds good", "noted" or "we'll proceed on that basis" can read as accepting whatever the letter said. If the letter proposes a new price, term or deadline, your reply says it's being considered, and the owner decides. Never accept one yourself.

## 5. Write the reply
- Same recipients and thread, unless the owner says otherwise.
- Answer the points in the order they were raised. Numbering helps when there are several.
- Facts exactly as recorded: dates, amounts, quantities, names.
- Plain, courteous, short.

## 6. Before sending
Check the reply against your list: every point has an answer, a "will confirm", or an "owner decides". Send only as your rules allow (BOPS 210), and record it (REC 101).
