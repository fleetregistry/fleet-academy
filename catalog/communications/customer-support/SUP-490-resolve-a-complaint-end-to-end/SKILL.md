# Resolve a Complaint End to End

This brings the major together. Each step is a course you've passed; this one is about doing them in order, with proof, and stopping where a person must decide.

## The path
1. **Read everything (SUP 120).** The whole message, the thread above it, the ticket history and the order. List every ask. *Proof: the list.*
2. **Is it yours?** Injury or safety, legal, press, privacy, fraud, distress, or outside the policy go to a person now, with a full handoff note. Stop here for that part. Don't do the routine part on your own unless your rules say you may.
3. **If it started in public (SOC 220),** reply publicly in two or three lines: acknowledge, move it private. No account details, no promises, no argument. *Proof: the reply's link.*
4. **Work out what's owed** from the written policy and the order: which items, which charges, minus what's already gone back. *Proof: the sum in one line.*
5. **Run the safety checks (SUP 450):** paid and not already refunded, no open dispute, original payment method, the account holder, within every limit, covered by the policy. Over a limit, or any check fails: stop, ask the person your rules name, issue nothing.
6. **Issue it once (SUP 410).** Wait for the confirmation. If the result is unclear, check the refund history before anything else. *Proof: the refund or credit ID.*
7. **Reply privately (SUP 210).** Answer every ask. The amount, where it's going, the timing the system gives. Keep any promise a colleague made. Send as your rules allow.
8. **Close the loop.** If it started in public, a short public follow-up only if your rules allow and it adds nothing private ("Glad we could sort this out"). Close the ticket only when what was owed is issued or handed to a person who has it. Record the chain: what came in, what you replied, what you issued, the IDs, who approved (REC 101).

## Rules for the whole path
- If a step fails, don't skip ahead. Fix it or hand it off.
- A new signal at any step (an injury mentioned in the second message, a chargeback appearing) sends you back to step 2.
- Never tell the customer something is done before the system confirms it.

## When to stop and ask
- Step 2, for any escalation signal.
- Step 5, above a limit or when a check fails.
- Step 6, when you can't tell whether a refund went through.
- Whenever the customer wants something the policy doesn't give.
