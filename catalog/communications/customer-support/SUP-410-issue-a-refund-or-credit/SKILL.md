# Issue a Refund or Credit

## 1. Work out what's owed
Start from the written policy and the order, not from what the customer asks for.
- **Which items:** only the items the policy covers. One broken plate in a set of six is one plate, unless the policy says otherwise.
- **Which charges:** item price, and shipping, tax or fees only if the policy says they're refunded.
- **Minus what's already gone back:** earlier refunds and credits on the order.
- Show the sum in one line: "$24.00 plate + $0 shipping (not refunded for returns under the policy) − $0 already refunded = $24.00."
If the customer asked for more than the policy gives, say so in your reply (SUP 210); don't round up.

## 2. Refund or credit
- Use what the customer chose, if the policy lets them choose.
- Offer store credit only where the policy allows it, and never push credit in place of a refund the policy owes.
- A credit's value and expiry are exactly what the policy says.

## 3. Run the safety checks
Every SUP 450 check must pass for this exact amount: paid and not already refunded, no open dispute, the original payment method, the account holder, within the limits, covered by the policy. If one fails, stop there.
Above your limit, issue nothing, not even the part under it. Ask the person your rules name to approve the full amount, with the order and your sum. Tell the customer it's waiting on approval, without promising the outcome or a date.

## 4. Issue it once
- Issue it in the payment or support system, against the order, to the original payment method.
- Wait for the system's confirmation (a refund ID or status).
- **If the result is unclear** (an error, a timeout, no confirmation), don't issue it again. Check the order's refund history first. If it's there, you're done. If it isn't, and you still can't tell, stop and ask a person. A retry on top of a refund that went through pays the customer twice.

## 5. Tell the customer
- The amount, and where it's going ("the card you paid with").
- The timing the system or policy gives ("refunds usually appear in 5 to 10 business days"). Never "instantly" or "today" unless the system says so.
- For a credit: the amount, how to use it, and when it expires.

## 6. Record it
What you issued, the amount, the order, the refund or credit ID, and which rule allowed it (REC 101).
