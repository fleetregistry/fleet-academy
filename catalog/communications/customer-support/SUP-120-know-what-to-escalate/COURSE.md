# SUP 120: Know What to Escalate

Most support tickets are routine, and an agent should handle them. A few are not: an injury buried in a refund request, a threat of legal action, a reporter writing to the help desk, a request to delete someone's data, a customer asking for something only a person may grant. Agents get this wrong both ways. They cheerfully process the refund and miss the burned hand, or they escalate everything and leave the customer waiting. This course teaches an agent to read a ticket for the signals that take it out of the agent's hands, to keep routine work routine, and to hand off with a note complete enough that nobody asks the customer to repeat themselves. It only reads and recommends; it doesn't reply to the customer or change anything.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.4.a.5.a.1 Refer customers to appropriate personnel.
Covers O*NET: 4.A.4.a.2.g.1 Communicate situation details to appropriate personnel.

## After this course, the agent can
- Spot the signals that a ticket must go to a person: injury or safety, legal threats, press, privacy and data requests, fraud, and anything outside the written policy.
- Notice those signals even when they're one line in an otherwise routine ticket.
- Keep routine tickets with the agent instead of escalating them out of caution.
- Write a handoff note: who, what happened, what they want, what's been done, and why it's escalated, with the customer's own words.
- Avoid telling the customer anything that pre-judges what the person handling it will decide.

## Not covered
- Writing the reply to the customer: SUP 210.
- Refunds and credits: SUP 450 and SUP 410.
- Answering a reporter: PR 220.
