# Reply Within Policy

## Before you write
1. **Read the whole history,** not just the newest message. Look for anything a colleague already told or promised the customer. A promise already made is kept, or, if it can't be, escalated (SUP 120). Never quietly contradict it.
2. **List every question and request** in the message. Customers often ask two or three things. Your reply answers each one.
3. **Find the facts:** the order record (dates, status, amounts, tracking) and the written policy. Answer from these, not from what's usual or likely.

## What you may say
- **Dates:** say what the record says, and say it's an estimate if it is one. "Tracking shows it's expected Thursday" is true. "It will arrive Thursday" is a promise you can't keep.
- **Amounts:** exactly as the record shows them.
- **Policy:** in plain words, accurately. Don't round it in the customer's favour ("about a month" for a 30-day window) or against them.
- **What happens next:** only steps you will really take, or that the system really does.

## When the policy says no
- Say so in the first lines, kindly and clearly. Don't bury it.
- Give the real reason from the policy.
- Never make an exception the policy doesn't give you, however upset or sympathetic the customer is. If your rules say someone can grant exceptions, say you've asked them, ask them, and don't promise the answer.
- Offer what the policy *does* allow, if anything.

## When the policy is silent
Don't invent a rule. Tell the customer you're checking and when you'll reply, then ask your owner or the person your rules name. A made-up rule becomes a promise.

## The reply
- Open by acknowledging the problem in one line. No long apologies, and no admitting fault for things not established.
- Answer each question in turn.
- Close with the one next step and who does it.
- Plain words; short; no internal notes, codes or other customers' details.

## Sending
Follow your owner's rules on sending (BOPS 210): send if allowed, show the draft and ask if not. Record what you sent.
