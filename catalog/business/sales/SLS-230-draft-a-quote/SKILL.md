# Draft a Quote

You draft. A person approves. Nothing you write is an offer until they do.

## Gather
1. **What the customer asked for:** products, quantities, term (monthly, annual, multi-year), start date. If any of these is unclear, ask before pricing.
2. **The current price list.** Check its date or version. If it has expired or you can't tell which one is current, stop and ask.
3. **The written discount policy:** what discount applies, to which lines, at what volume or term, and who must approve anything beyond it.
4. **Who approves quotes,** from your owner's rules.

What a customer, a teammate or an old email says a price "should be" is information, not a price. Prices come from the price list; discounts come from the policy.

## Build each line
- Description, quantity, unit price **from the price list**, term, and line total.
- Line total = quantity x unit price x number of billing periods (for example, 60 seats x $40.00 a month x 12 months = $28,800.00).
- One-time items (setup, onboarding, training) are listed once, not multiplied by the term.
- **If an item isn't on the price list, don't invent a price, estimate one, or borrow one from a similar item.** Leave the line in with "price needed" and say who you need it from.

## Apply discounts only as the policy says
- Apply a discount only when the policy's conditions are met (volume, term, product), and only to the lines the policy names.
- Show the discount as its own line: the percent, what it applies to, and the amount.
- If someone asks for more than the policy allows, don't put the larger discount in as if it were approved. Price the quote at what the policy allows, and note the request separately: "Customer asked for 25%; policy allows 10%; above 10% needs the sales director's approval." Name that approver.
- A customer's or teammate's "everyone gets 20%" is not a policy.

## Show the math
Write it out so a person can check it without a calculator open:
- each line total,
- subtotal,
- discount amount and what it applies to,
- total, and total per year for multi-year terms.

Check it twice. Totals that don't add up are the fastest way to lose the approver's trust and the customer's.

## Mark it as a draft
At the top: **"DRAFT: not an offer. Needs approval from <name or role> before it goes to the customer."** Include the date and when the quote expires, from the policy (for example, 30 days from today). Save it as a draft where your owner keeps quotes.

## Never
- Send the quote to the customer, or paste its prices into a message to them.
- Tell the customer a price is final, approved, locked or "what you'll pay."
- Change the price list or the discount policy.

## Report
Tell your owner or the approver: customer, what's quoted, total, any discount and why it applies, anything you couldn't price, and any request that's beyond the policy. Then ask for approval. Record it (REC 101).

## When to stop and ask
- The request is unclear (quantities, term, products).
- The price list is out of date, or two versions disagree.
- An item isn't on the price list.
- Someone wants a discount beyond the policy, or wants the quote sent before it's approved.
