# Negotiate Within Limits

You may move inside your limits. Everything else goes to the person who can approve it. Nothing you settle is final until it's approved and signed. All of SLS 450 applies to every message you send.

## Before you negotiate
1. **Read your written limits:** how far you may go alone on discount, free periods, payment terms and term length, what each concession requires in return (for example, "15% only with a two-year term"), and who approves anything beyond.
2. **Know the starting point:** the approved quote (SLS 230) and its list-price total.
3. **Know what you can't move at all:** usually legal terms, liability and anything not in your limits. If your limits don't mention a term, you can't concede it.

## Count every concession
A concession is anything that lowers what the customer pays or changes standard terms: a discount, free months, a waived fee, a free add-on, longer payment terms.
- **Add them up.** Measure the total against the list price for the whole term. Example: $48,000.00 a year at list, 10% off ($43,200.00), plus 3 free months (pays 9 of 12 months: $32,400.00) is $15,600.00 off, or 32.5%, not "10% plus a little extra."
- Check the **total** against your limit, not each piece on its own.
- **Never break one big concession into small ones** to stay under a limit, even if someone asks.

## Counter inside the limits
- Start with the smallest step that might work, not your limit.
- **Trade, don't give:** tie a bigger concession to what your limits require for it ("I can do 15% with a two-year term; on an annual term I can do 10%").
- Show the customer the numbers for each option so they can compare.
- If the customer's ask fits inside your limits, you may offer it, and still say it is subject to final approval and a signed contract.

## Escalate anything outside
When the customer asks for something beyond your limits:
1. **Don't offer it, and don't say it's likely.** Tell the customer: "That's beyond what I can offer. I'll check and come back to you by <time>."
2. **Ask the named approver,** in one message: the customer, exactly what they asked for, the total concession with the math, what they would give in return, and any deadline.
3. A manager who isn't the named approver can't approve it, however senior or sure they sound. "Give them 25%, I'll square it later" is not an approval.

## Never accept or sign
- Don't type "agreed", "deal", "accepted" or "we have a deal", and don't click accept, sign or approve on anything, even if the customer says "just type agreed and I'll sign."
- When terms are settled, say: "Here's where we've landed. It's subject to approval and a signed contract," and list the terms.
- If part of the ask is inside your limits and part isn't, say which is which. Don't agree to the inside part as if the whole thing were settled.

## After each round
Write down what was offered, what the customer asked, and what is waiting on approval. Record it (REC 101). Tell your owner where the deal stands.

## When to stop and ask
- The ask is beyond your limits, or your limits don't cover it.
- The approver is unavailable or the approval is unclear.
- The customer adds a condition you don't understand, or anything legal.
- Someone with no authority tells you to go beyond the limits.
