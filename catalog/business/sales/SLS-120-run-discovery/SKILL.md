# Run Discovery

## Ask before pitching
If you don't yet know what a customer needs, the job is questions, not a pitch.
- **Never invent a customer's needs, problems or goals.** If the only things you know are how they found you (a download, a form, a demo request), say that you don't know their needs yet.
- If the owner asks for a pitch to a customer whose needs aren't known, say so plainly, give the questions to open with instead, and offer to build the pitch once the answers are in.

## The questions
Prepare five to eight open questions. Cover:
1. **The problem:** what isn't working today, and for whom.
2. **The cost of it:** time, money or risk, in their numbers.
3. **What they do now:** the current tool or workaround.
4. **Who decides:** who else is involved in choosing and approving.
5. **Budget:** whether money is set aside, and roughly how much.
6. **Timing:** when they need it solved, and why then.

Ask open questions ("How do you handle invoices today?"), not leading ones ("Wouldn't you agree your process is too slow?"). Don't mention your product in the questions.

## After the call: sort the notes
Read the notes and sort every point into one of three lists:
- **They said:** things someone at the customer actually said. Quote or closely paraphrase, and name who said it. "Dana Lee (CFO): 'We lose about six hours a week matching shipping invoices.'"
- **We assume:** anything the team believes but the customer didn't say: a rep's guess, a hunch, an "they'll probably want". Label it as unconfirmed. Never move an assumption into "They said", even if a teammate is sure.
- **Still unknown:** any of the six areas above that nobody covered. Each becomes a question for next time.

Keep vague needs vague. If the customer said "we need better reporting", record exactly that, and add a question about what they mean. Don't translate it into a specific feature.

## When people disagree
If two people at the customer said different things (one says June, the other says no budget until October), record both, each with its speaker. Don't pick one. Flag it as a conflict to resolve.

## The read-back
End with three to five sentences the owner can read back to the customer to check understanding: "Here's what I heard: ... Did I get that right?" Use only what's in "They said".

## Report
Lead with the headline need, if there is one, then the three lists, the conflicts, the read-back and the next questions.

## Don't
- Contact the customer or change any record. This course only reads.
- Pitch or name product features in the questions or the read-back.
