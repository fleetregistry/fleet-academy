# Build the Pitch

You need three things before you start: the customer's stated needs (from discovery, SLS 120), the product facts, and the approved proof (customer stories, quotes and numbers the owner is allowed to share). If you don't have the needs, ask for them first. A pitch without them is guesswork.

## The pitch
1. **Opening:** the customer's situation in their own words. "You told us your team loses six hours a week matching invoices."
2. **One section per need:** the need, then how the product answers it, then the proof. Every main point names the need it answers. A point that doesn't answer a stated need is cut.
3. **Proof:** only approved customer stories and figures, exactly as approved.
4. **Price,** if asked for: only from the current price list.
5. **Next step:** one clear ask.

Keep it short: one point per slide or paragraph.

## Every claim needs a fact
- Each product claim must come from the product facts. Use the facts' own limits and numbers.
- If a stated need isn't met by the product, **don't stretch a feature to cover it.** Leave it out of the pitch, and tell the owner plainly which need isn't met and why, so they can decide how to handle it.

## Proof you may use
- Use a customer's name, logo, quote or numbers only if the approved proof says you may use them publicly.
- If a story is marked internal only, or permission isn't recorded, don't name the customer. Tell the owner the story exists and that permission is needed.
- **Never invent** customer logos, customer names, statistics, quotes, awards, rankings or "customers typically save X%". If someone asks you to add them, don't, and tell them what approved proof you can use instead.

## Savings and return on investment
- Calculate only from the customer's own numbers (their hours, their costs, their volumes) and the product facts. Show the math.
- If you don't have the customer's numbers, don't make them up and don't use an industry average. Leave a marked gap ("[need: hourly cost of the AP team]") and tell the owner what to ask for.

## Hand it over
Give the owner the draft and a short list: needs the pitch covers, needs it doesn't, anything that needs permission or a number. The pitch is a draft; don't send or publish it.
