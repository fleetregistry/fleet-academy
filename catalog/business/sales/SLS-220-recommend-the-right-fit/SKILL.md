# Recommend the Right Fit

## What you need
- **The customer's must-haves:** what they said they need (from discovery, SLS 120), including how many people or units.
- **Their budget,** if they gave one.
- **The price list and product facts:** what each option includes, its limits (user caps, regions, hosting) and its price.

## Steps
1. **List the must-haves** exactly as the customer stated them.
2. **Check every option against every must-have.** An option fits only if it meets all of them, including limits like user caps. Use the product facts, never what an option "probably" includes.
3. **Recommend the smallest option that fits.** If a cheaper tier meets every must-have, recommend it, even if a bigger tier would be a bigger sale and even if someone asks you to push the bigger one. You may mention what the bigger tier adds, as an option, but say the customer didn't ask for it.
4. **Work out the price exactly.** Seats times price per seat, per month or per year as the price list states. Show the math: "8 users x $20 = $160 per month."
5. **Compare with the budget.** Say whether it fits, and if not, by how much.

## When it doesn't fit
- **Over budget:** say so with the numbers (the price, the budget, the gap). Don't offer a discount, free months or special terms unless the price list allows them without approval. If discounts need someone's approval, say who, and leave the decision to the owner. Don't drop a must-have to make the price fit unless the customer chooses to.
- **Nothing fits:** if no option meets a must-have (they need something the product doesn't do), say plainly that it isn't a fit, and why. Don't hint that it can be arranged or built. A clear "not a fit" saves everyone's time and keeps trust.

## When the need is vague
If a must-have could mean different things that point to different options ("it needs to be secure" when one tier has single sign-on and a higher one adds audit logs), don't guess. Rule out any option that fails on what you do know, then ask what the customer means, and give the price of each remaining option.

## Report
Lead with the recommendation and its price, then the check against each must-have, then the budget comparison, then anything that needs the owner (an approval, a question for the customer). Don't send it to the customer or agree a price; give it to the owner.
