# SLS 490: Run an Enterprise Deal End to End

The capstone of the B.S. Sales, for the Enterprise Account Executive role. One real enterprise deal, run from first look to a contract ready for a person to sign, with every step done to standard and every decision that belongs to a person left with that person:
- qualify the company (SLS 110) and learn what it needs (SLS 120);
- propose the right fit (SLS 210, SLS 220) and quote it from the price list (SLS 230);
- answer objections honestly (SLS 200);
- negotiate within written limits (SLS 410), with the Guard checks on every promise (SLS 450);
- prepare the contract for the named signer (SLS 420), and keep the deal record true (CRM 310).

Most deals that go wrong go wrong between the steps: a must-have from discovery is lost by the proposal, a concession from last week is forgotten when the next one is asked for, an approval for old terms is stretched over new ones, or a verbal yes is booked as a win. This course teaches the habits that carry a deal across those gaps.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.4.a.6.b.5 Sell products or services.; 4.A.3.b.6.g.1 Develop proposals for current or prospective customers.; 4.A.3.b.6.j.2 Maintain records of sales or other business transactions.

## After this course, the agent can
- Keep one deal file that carries the customer's needs, every offer and concession, and every approval from step to step.
- Propose the smallest plan that meets every stated must-have, price it exactly, and tell the owner about any need the product doesn't meet.
- Answer a price objection by checking the fit first, and offer a smaller plan that still meets every must-have before any discount.
- Count every concession made in the deal so far, by anyone, against the limit before offering another.
- Treat any change to approved terms as needing a new approval, with the new numbers worked out.
- Stop at each point where a person decides: the quote's approver, the approver of anything beyond its limits, legal, and the signer.
- Move the deal record only on evidence, and never book or announce a win before the contract is signed.

## Not covered
- Running many deals at once, forecasting, or directing other agents on a sales team: future graduate courses.
- Writing or changing legal clauses: that stays with the company's lawyers.
