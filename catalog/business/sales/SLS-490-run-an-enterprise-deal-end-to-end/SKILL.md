# Run an Enterprise Deal End to End

Every step is a course you've passed. This one is about doing them in order, carrying the facts forward, and stopping where a person decides. Deals usually go wrong between steps: a need from discovery is lost, last week's concession is forgotten, an approval for old terms is stretched over new ones.

## Keep a deal file
One running record for the deal, where your owner keeps deals. Read all of it before each step, and add to it after:
- what the customer said they need (must-haves, number of users, budget, timing), and who said it,
- every offer and concession so far, by anyone, with its amount,
- every approval: who, when, and the exact terms it covers,
- what is waiting on whom.

## The path
1. **Qualify (SLS 110)** against the owner's profile.
2. **Discover (SLS 120).** No proposal before the needs are known.
3. **Propose (SLS 210, SLS 220).** Recommend the smallest plan that meets every must-have, not the biggest, even if someone wants a bigger sale. Tie each point to a stated need. Tell the owner plainly about any need the product doesn't meet.
4. **Quote (SLS 230)** from the price list and the discount policy, with the math shown. *Stop: until the named approver says yes, it is a draft and nothing goes to the customer.*
5. **Answer objections (SLS 200)** from the product facts only.
6. **Negotiate (SLS 410)** inside the written limits. *Stop: anything beyond them goes to the named approver.*
7. **Prepare the contract (SLS 420)** from the template and the approved terms. *Stop: the named signer signs, never you.*
8. **Keep the record true (CRM 310).** Move the deal stage only on evidence.

## Price objections: check the fit before you discount
When the customer says it costs too much, look at the deal file first. Does the proposal include more than they asked for? If a smaller plan meets every must-have, offer it at list price with the math. That isn't a concession; it's the right fit. Only then consider a discount, inside your limits. Don't drop a must-have to make the price fit unless the customer chooses to.

## Count every concession in the whole deal
Before offering anything, add every concession already in the deal file (discounts, waived fees, free months, made by you or anyone else) to the new one, and measure the total against the list price for the whole deal. Check that total against your limit. A fee someone waived last week still counts. If the total is over, counter with what still fits, or ask the approver with the math.

## A change means a new approval
An approval covers the exact terms it names. If anything changes after it (seats, plan, term, start date, payment terms), work out the new numbers, ask the approver for a yes on the new terms, and keep the change out of any quote or contract until they give it. Tell the customer you're checking, without saying yes.

## Won means signed
"We're good to go", "I'll sign next week", or the customer's signature still waiting on your company's, is progress, not a win. Log it as an activity and leave the stage where it is. Move the deal to won only when your owner's rules allow (usually the contract signed by both sides), and don't announce a win before then, whoever asks and however close the quarter end is. Tell the person asking what is still missing.

## Every message to the customer
Say that what you offer is subject to final approval and a signed contract. Never type "agreed" or accept for the company (SLS 450).

## Report
After each step, tell your owner where the deal stands: what happened, the numbers, what's waiting on whom, and the next stop point. Record it (REC 101).
