# Prepare the Contract

You assemble. A person signs. All of SLS 450 applies before anything goes to the customer or the signer.

## Gather
1. **The approved terms,** in writing, from the named approver: customer, products, quantities, price, discount, total, term length, start date, payment terms, and anything non-standard. Approved means what SLS 450 says: the right person, this deal, these terms.
2. **The current standard template.** Check its version. If you can't tell which is current, stop and ask.
3. **Who signs,** from your owner's rules, and anyone who must review first (for example, legal for any change to a legal clause).

## Check the terms agree before you start
Compare the approval with the deal record and the last offer. If any of them disagree (different term length, quantity, price or payment terms), stop and ask the approver which is right. Don't pick one yourself, even if one looks like a typo.

## Assemble
- Use the standard template. Fill only its blanks (names, products, quantities, prices, dates, terms) with the approved terms.
- **Don't add anything** that wasn't agreed and approved, and **don't drop anything** that was.
- **Don't write or rewrite legal clauses** (liability, indemnity, renewal, termination, confidentiality). If a deal needs one changed, that goes to the person your rules name for legal review.
- Check the numbers: each line total, the total for the term, and that they match the approved total to the cent.

## List every deviation
Any place the contract differs from the standard template is a deviation: a non-standard payment term, a custom discount, a changed or added clause, a different renewal or cancellation term. Put them in a short list at the top of your note:
- what differs from the template,
- who approved it, and when.

If a deviation has no approval, it doesn't go in the contract. Leave the template's standard wording, and ask the approver.

If there are no deviations, say so: "No changes from the standard template."

## Customer redlines
When the customer sends back a marked-up contract:
- **Never accept their changes into the contract yourself,** even small ones, even if they say "these are just cleanups."
- List each change: the clause, what the template says, what they want instead.
- Send the list to the approver, and to legal for any legal clause. Tell the customer you're reviewing their changes, without agreeing to any.

## Read it back
Before routing, re-read the finished contract against the approved terms, line by line. Any difference: fix it to match the approval, or stop and ask.

## Route for signature, never sign
- Send it to the **named signer** with: the customer, the total, the term, and the deviation list.
- **Never sign, e-sign, type a signature, click "accept" or "agree", or use anyone's signing account,** even if asked, even if they say the signer already said yes out loud.
- If the signer is unavailable, wait or ask your owner. Don't route it to someone else to sign without your owner naming them.
- Record it (REC 101): customer, version of the template, deviations and who approved them, who it was routed to and when.

## When to stop and ask
- The approved terms disagree with each other or with the deal record.
- A deviation or a redline has no approval.
- Anyone asks you to sign, or to send a contract as if it were signed.
- The template version is unclear.
