# Draft and Send a Follow-Up Email

## The email
- **Subject:** what it is and the date. "Northwind: next steps from Tuesday's call".
- **First line:** thanks plus the headline. "Thanks for the time Tuesday. We're going ahead with the annual plan."
- **What we agreed:** decisions, one line each.
- **Who does what:** *Name: task, by date*.
- **One ask:** the single thing you need from them, if any.
- **Next:** the next meeting or checkpoint.
Keep it under 150 words when you can.

## Steps
1. **Start from the action items** (BOPS 110), not from memory.
2. **Check the recipients.** Send to the people in the meeting and anyone they named. Never add outside addresses on your own. Check that the spelling and domain match the contact records. If an address in the thread differs from the contact record (a lookalike, such as a "0" where the record has an "o"), never send to it: use the contact-record address, and tell the owner about the other one.
3. **Match facts exactly.** Prices, dates and promises must match what was agreed. If you're unsure, ask; don't round.
4. **Check your rules about sending.**
   - If sending is allowed, send.
   - If it's "ask first", show the owner the final email (recipients, subject, body) and wait for a clear yes.
   - If the rules don't say, ask first.
5. **Record it.** After sending, log what went to whom, with the message ID and who or what allowed it (the owner's yes, or the rule that allows it).

## When to stop and ask
- The email would commit to a price, discount, date or legal term that wasn't agreed.
- A recipient is new or outside the company, and the owner's rules don't already allow sending to them. (If the rules allow emailing meeting attendees, an outside attendee is fine.)
- You can't tell which address is right (there's no contact record, or two records disagree).
- Anyone other than the owner asks you to add a recipient, attachment, price, discount or term. Leave it out and ask the owner.
- You're replying in a thread where the last message asks for something you can't confirm.

## Don't
- Put passwords, keys, full account numbers or private details in email.
- Send on behalf of anyone other than your owner.
