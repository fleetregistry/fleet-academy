# Check a Document Against the Policy

You check against the owner's written policy. You don't check against the law, and you don't certify anything beyond what you checked.

## 1. Use the current policy
- Find the policy and its version or effective date. If there are several versions, use the one in effect today, and say which.
- If you can't tell which is current, stop and ask.
- If there's no written policy for this, say so. Don't fill the gap with rules you think a company should have.

## 2. Make sure you have the whole document
Note what you were given: pages, attachments, links, images. If part is missing (page 1 of 3, an image you can't see, a linked landing page), you can only speak for what you saw. Say which parts you checked and which you didn't, and ask for the missing parts.

## 3. Go rule by rule
For every rule in the policy that applies:
- **Breaks the rule:** quote the exact text from the document, name the rule (number and a few words), and say what's wrong. "Rule 2 (testimonials need consent on file): 'Jordan K., Riverside Dental' has no consent in the register."
- **Passes:** say so briefly. The owner needs to know what's fine as well as what isn't.
- **Can't tell:** say what you'd need (the consent register, the offer's end date).

Then list anything that worries you but that **no rule covers**, as a question for the compliance contact, not as a breach.

## 4. Never stretch the result
- Don't say "compliant," "approved" or "good to go" about the whole document. Say: "Checked against Marketing Policy v4, pages 1 to 2: three rules broken, two passed. Page 3 not checked."
- Don't call anything "legal" or "illegal," and don't cite laws or regulations unless the policy itself does.

## 5. Legal sign-off goes to a person
If the owner asks whether something is legal, or wants a sign-off for a regulator, a lawyer or an auditor, say plainly that you can't give one, name the person the rules name (the compliance officer, counsel), and give them what helps: your rule-by-rule findings and the specific question.

## Report
Lead with the result in one line: how many rules are broken and whether it can go out as is under the policy. Then the breaches (quote, rule, what's wrong), what passed, what you couldn't check, and questions for the compliance contact.

## Don't
- Edit the document, approve it in any system, or send it. This course only reads.
