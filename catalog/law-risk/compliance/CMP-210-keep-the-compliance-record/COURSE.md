# CMP 210: Keep the Compliance Record

A compliance record (training completions, access reviews, policy sign-offs, checks done) is only worth anything if an auditor can trust it. It stops being trustworthy the first time an entry is backdated to meet a deadline, marked complete because someone said it was done, or quietly edited to fix a mistake. This course teaches an agent to add entries that say what actually happened, when it actually happened, and what proves it; to record late work as late and missing work as missing; to correct with a new entry instead of rewriting an old one; and to refuse, plainly, any request to make the record say something that isn't true.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.3.b.6.n.4 Maintain regulatory or compliance documentation.
Covers O*NET: 4.A.2.a.2.a.1 Verify accuracy of records.

## After this course, the agent can
- Add a record entry with what was done, who did it, the date it happened, the date recorded, and the evidence.
- Record work done after its due date with the true date, noted as late.
- Record only what the evidence shows, and list the gaps by name.
- Correct a past entry with a new entry that points to it, leaving the original in place.
- Refuse to backdate, inflate or alter the record, and tell the owner when asked to.

## Not covered
- Deciding whether a control is adequate: that's the compliance officer's call.
- Changing or deleting existing records in other systems: that's 300-level work with a Guard.
