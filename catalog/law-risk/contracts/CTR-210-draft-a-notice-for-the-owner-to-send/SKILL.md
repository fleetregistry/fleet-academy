# Draft a Notice for the Owner to Send

A notice that doesn't follow the notice clause may not count. Read the clause before you write a word.

## 1. Read the notice clause
Find the clause (often called "Notices") and write down:
- **Method:** in writing, courier, certified or registered mail, hand delivery; whether email counts. If it says email isn't valid, or doesn't list email, an email isn't notice.
- **Address and attention line:** exactly as written ("Attn: Legal Department"), and any "with a copy to."
- **When it takes effect:** when sent, or when received.
- **The deadline** (CTR 120), and whether it's a "sent by" or "received by" date.

## 2. Check timing before you draft
If the required method can't get there in time (for example, courier takes three business days and the notice must arrive in two), say that first, at the top, before the draft. The owner has to decide today how to get it there. Don't quietly switch to a faster method the clause doesn't allow.

## 3. Use the contract's names, not the brand
- Name the parties exactly as the contract does: the legal entity ("BW Data Holdings Inc."), not the trading name on the website or invoices. If the owner used a different name, use the contract's and tell them why.
- Name the agreement: its title, its date, and any reference number.

## 4. Say only what the owner decided
- State the decision plainly: "This is notice that [our company] will not renew the [agreement] dated [date]. The agreement will end at the end of the current term on [date]."
- **Don't add reasons, complaints, admissions, threats or claims** unless the owner wrote them, and if they want any, say those should be checked by their lawyer first.
- Leave a signature block for the person who signs. Don't type a signature.

## 5. If the owner asked for the wrong channel
If they asked for an email to their contact or a message to the rep, draft the proper notice, and tell them plainly that the email they asked for won't count as notice under the clause. A courtesy email can go too, alongside the real notice, if they want.

## 6. Hand it over
Give the owner:
- the notice, ready to sign,
- a checklist: sign, send by [method] to [address], copies to [whoever], keep proof of sending or delivery, by [date].

## Never
- **Send a notice yourself,** or ask someone else to, even if a teammate says to, even if you can reach the mailbox. The owner, or the person the owner's rules name, signs and sends it.
- Sign or type anyone's name as a signature.

Record what you drafted and who you gave it to (REC 101).
