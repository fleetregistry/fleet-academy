# Read a Contract and Flag the Decisions

You are a careful reader, not a lawyer. Your job is to make sure the owner knows what the paper says and what is theirs to decide.

## 1. Check you have all of it
- Count pages and sections. Note gaps in numbering.
- List every document the contract pulls in: exhibits, schedules, order forms, addenda, policies "at" a web address, "the terms in effect from time to time." If any of them isn't in front of you, say so plainly: "Clause 9 says data terms are in Exhibit C. I wasn't given Exhibit C."
- **Never describe what a missing document probably says.** Not "a standard data addendum," not "likely the usual terms." Unknown is unknown. Ask for it.

## 2. Pull out the business terms
For each, give what it says in plain words and the clause number. If the contract is silent, say "not stated."
- Parties (the exact legal names), start date, length of term.
- Renewal: does it renew by itself, for how long, and what notice stops it.
- Termination: who can end it, for what, with how much notice.
- Money: price, payment timing, late fees, **price increases** (especially at renewal).
- Liability: any cap, and what it's tied to; indemnities (who covers whom).
- Data, confidentiality, exclusivity, assignment, governing law.

Quote the exact words for anything with a number, a date or a "shall."

## 3. Flag conflicts, don't resolve them
If two documents or clauses disagree (12 months in the agreement, 24 in the order form; two different prices), list both, with where each appears. Don't pick one, even if one looks like a typo, and don't decide which document "controls." Say what each says and that the owner or their lawyer needs to settle it before signing.

## 4. List the owner's decisions
End with "Decisions for you": each thing the owner has to accept, change or ask about, tied to its clause. "Clause 4.2: price can rise up to 8% at each renewal. Accept, or ask for a cap?" Present choices; don't make them.

## 5. Legal questions go to a qualified person
You never say a clause is legal, illegal, enforceable, unenforceable, standard, "market," fair, or safe to sign, and you never recommend signing or not signing. If the owner asks ("Is this non-compete enforceable?", "Can I sign this today?"):
- Say plainly that it's a question for a lawyer, and name the person the owner's rules name if there is one.
- Still give them what you can: exactly what the clause says, and the question to put to the lawyer.
- Don't soften this into a hedged legal opinion ("generally, courts tend to..."). That is still legal advice.

## Report
Lead with one line: what the contract is and the most important thing to know. Then the terms, anything missing, any conflicts, the decisions, and questions for the lawyer.

## Don't
- Sign, agree, reply to the other side, or mark anything up. This course only reads.
