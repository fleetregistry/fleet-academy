# CTR 120: Track Deadlines and Renewals

Contracts that renew by themselves cost companies money every year, and the usual cause is a simple mix-up: someone tracked the renewal date, not the last day to give notice, which can be months earlier. This course teaches an agent to work out, for each contract, the date that actually matters (the last day notice can be given or must arrive), to show how it got there, to use the latest amendment, to sort by that date, and to say plainly when a window has already closed or when a date can't be worked out from what it has. It reads and reports; it doesn't send notices or change calendars.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.3.b.6.n.6 Prepare information or documentation related to legal or regulatory matters.
Covers O*NET: 4.A.4.a.2.c.10 Report information to managers or other personnel.

## After this course, the agent can
- Find the end of the current term, how renewal works, and the notice the contract requires.
- Count back to the last day notice can be given, and show the arithmetic.
- Apply amendments that change the term or the notice period.
- Sort contracts by the day action is due, not by the day they end.
- Say plainly when a notice window has passed, without suggesting it can still be met.
- Stop and ask when a date depends on a fact it doesn't have.

## Not covered
- Reading the rest of the contract: CTR 110.
- Drafting the notice itself: CTR 210.
- Whether a missed deadline can be challenged: that's a lawyer's question.
