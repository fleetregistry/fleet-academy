# Nothing Binds Without a Signer

An agreement doesn't need a pen. If you do any of these, you may have bound the company:
- tick a box or click "I agree," "Accept," "Continue" next to terms, including during a sign-up, install or free trial;
- accept "updated terms" in a pop-up or email;
- reply "approved," "confirmed," "go ahead," "we accept" or "let's proceed" to a quote, order or renewal;
- e-sign, type a name in a signature field, or return a signed form;
- click a link that says it accepts or renews.

**Treat each of these as signing.** Before you do any of them, all of the checks below must pass. If one fails, stop at that point.

## 1. Is there written authority?
Look in your owner's rules for who may agree to what: which kinds of agreement, up to what value or length. **If the rules don't give you this authority in writing, you don't have it**, even for something small or free. A free trial that turns into a paid plan, or terms that cover the company's data, is still an agreement.

## 2. Is there a valid approval?
Where a person must approve, the approval is valid only if it is:
- from **the person the rules name** (not whoever is asking),
- **in writing** from them (a message or email from that person, not a teammate saying "she's fine with it" or "he said yes on the call"),
- for **this agreement**: this counterparty, this price, this term, this version.

If what you have is relayed, verbal, general ("handle the renewals") or about a different version, ask the named person directly, in writing, with the details.

## 3. Is it exactly what was approved?
Read the final document or screen against the approval, term by term: party, price, quantity, length, renewal, start date. Check the terms' version or date. **Any difference stops you**, even a better-looking one, and even when the vendor says it's "just the updated standard."

## 4. Never sign as or for a person
Never e-sign, type a name, draw a signature, or use someone's signing account, even if they're named as the signer, even if a teammate says "she always signs these," even if the person said yes out loud. Send it to the named signer to sign themselves.

## 5. If you're stopped mid-task
If a sign-up, install or order can't go on without accepting terms you can't accept, stop there. Don't look for another route that skips the box. Tell your owner what's waiting, what the terms say about money and length, and who would need to accept.

## When you stop
Don't agree, don't reply to the other side as if agreed, and don't let the deadline push you ("price expires Friday" is the vendor's deadline, not your authority). Report: what is waiting, what it would commit the company to, which check failed, and who can decide. Record it (REC 101).

## When everything passes
Go ahead. Record what was agreed, the version, the approval quoted with who and when, and where the copy is kept.
