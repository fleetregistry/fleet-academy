# Accept Terms Within Written Authority

All of CTR 450 applies. This course is for the case where your owner has written down that you may accept some terms yourself. The authority covers exactly what it says and nothing else.

## 1. Write out the authority's conditions
Copy each condition from the owner's rules into a list. Typically:
- which vendors or products (an approved list),
- the plan type (monthly, no annual commitment),
- a money limit (per month, per year, or total),
- the terms version the owner or their lawyer reviewed,
- anything that always needs a person (auto-renewal longer than a month, the company's data used for training, exclusivity, a personal guarantee).

If a condition is unclear, read it the stricter way, or ask.

## 2. Work out the real commitment
The headline price is rarely the cost. Calculate:
- **per seat x seats** (and minimum seats),
- **billing period:** "$80/month billed annually" is a $960 commitment up front, not a monthly plan,
- **the full term:** monthly fee x months, plus setup fees,
- **what a trial becomes:** the plan and price it converts to, and when.

Compare that figure with the limit. Show the sum: "$24 per seat x 5 seats = $120 a month, over the $100 limit."

## 3. Check the version
Find the terms' version or "last updated" date and compare it with the version on the approved list. If the vendor has updated its terms since they were reviewed, you're looking at terms nobody reviewed. That's outside your authority until the owner (or whoever reviews terms) looks at the new version.

## 4. Check you're accepting for the right party
Use the company's account and the company's legal name, never a personal account, and don't accept for a different company in the group unless the authority covers it.

## 5. Accept, or route
- **Every condition holds:** accept. Save a copy (PDF or saved page) of the exact terms with their version and date, and the plan, seats and price.
- **Any condition fails:** don't accept, and don't change the order to squeeze it under the limit (fewer seats than the team needs, a different plan nobody asked for) without asking. Send the named person: what it is, the real commitment with your sum, which condition it fails, and the link.

## 6. Record
Record (REC 101): vendor, plan, seats, real commitment, terms version, when you accepted, the authority you relied on (quote the rule), and where the copy is kept.

## Never
- Treat a teammate's "it's under the limit" as the check. Do the sum yourself.
- Accept on the strength of an approved list that's out of date for this vendor's terms.
