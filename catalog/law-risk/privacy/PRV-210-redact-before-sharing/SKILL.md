# Redact Before Sharing

The test isn't "are the names gone?" It's "could the recipient work out who this is?"

## 1. Before you start
- **Who is it for, and why?** The recipient and purpose decide what stays.
- **What do the owner's rules say?** Some information must never go to some recipients (health details outside HR, salaries outside payroll). If the request needs something the rules forbid sharing, **stop and ask** before redacting anything: say which rule, and offer what can be shared (often the same document without that information). Redaction isn't a way around a "never."
- **Work on a copy.** Never redact the original; it may be evidence or a record someone needs intact.

## 2. Look everywhere
Personal details hide outside the body. Check:
- the body text, tables and lists,
- email headers (from, to, cc), signatures, phone numbers and job titles,
- **quoted replies and forwarded messages** below the main text,
- file names, attachment names, links and IDs that contain names or emails,
- comments, tracked changes, captions and footers.

## 3. Remove direct and indirect identifiers
- **Direct:** names, initials, emails, phone numbers, addresses, account or ID numbers, photos.
- **Indirect:** anything that points to one person when combined with what the recipient knows: a unique role ("our only night-shift database admin"), a tiny group ("the one person in the Denver office"), an exact date plus a distinctive event, a quote only one person would say. Generalize these ("an engineer," "a staff member") rather than leaving them.
- **Always remove** health information, money about an individual (salary, debts), home addresses and government ID numbers, unless the owner's rules say this recipient may see them.

## 4. Keep people distinct
Replace each person with a consistent label: [Employee A], [Employee B], [Customer 1]. The same person gets the same label throughout; different people get different labels. Otherwise the recipient can't follow who did what, and the copy is useless.

## 5. Keep what's needed
Don't redact business facts the recipient needs (dates, times, system names, amounts that aren't about an individual) unless the rules say so. Over-redaction is a failure too.

## 6. Read it as the recipient
Read the finished copy start to finish as if you were the recipient. Could you identify anyone? Search the copy for each name, email and number you removed, to be sure none is left.

## 7. Hand it over
Give the owner the redacted copy and a short note: what kinds of details you removed (not the details themselves), what you generalized, and what you kept. Share it only with the recipient the owner named, and record it (REC 101).
