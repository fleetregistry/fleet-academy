# Before You Delete Personal Data

A deletion can't be undone. Before any record is deleted or changed, all of these must be true. If any one isn't, stop and ask.

## 1. The request is verified and approved
- The request was verified under the owner's process (PRV 220), and you can say how and when.
- The person the process names (for example the privacy lead) has approved this deletion **in writing**, for this person and these systems. A teammate's "just delete it" is not approval.

## 2. You have the right person, and only them
- Match records by **unique identifiers** from the verified request: account ID, the verified email. Never by name alone.
- If a search turns up records that share the name but not the identifiers (a different email, city, account), they belong to someone else. **Leave them alone** and note them as out of scope.
- If you can't tell whether a record is theirs, it stays, and you ask.

## 3. Holds and retention come before deletion
- Check the **legal hold list.** If the person or their records are on it (a dispute, an investigation), nothing under the hold is deleted. Tell the privacy lead; they decide how to answer.
- Check the **retention schedule.** Records the company must keep (for example invoices for 7 years) are kept, and handled the way the process says (restricted, not deleted).
- Don't decide yourself that a hold or retention rule doesn't apply, or that the request "overrides" it.

## 4. Preview, and a second look
- List exactly what will be deleted or changed: system, record ID, type, count. And what will be kept, and why.
- Get the named person to confirm that list. The approval covers that list and nothing more.

## 5. Stop if anything changed
Just before you act, run the same search again. If the count or the records differ from the approved list (new records synced in, a record changed), **stop**. Delete nothing beyond the approved list until a person has looked at the difference.

## 6. Corrections
For a correction, change only the field asked, from the old value to the verified new value, and keep the old value where the process says, so it can be audited.

## 7. Record without the data
Record (REC 101): the request ID, who approved and when, which systems, how many records deleted or kept and why. **Don't write the person's name, email or other details into the log.** The log of a deletion mustn't become a copy of what was deleted.
