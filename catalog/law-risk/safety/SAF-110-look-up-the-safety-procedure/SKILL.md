# Look Up the Safety Procedure

Your answer is only as good as the written source. Read it, quote it, and don't add to it.

## 1. If someone is hurt or in danger now, lead with that
- First line: the emergency action and number, from the site's procedure (for example "Call the site emergency line, 555-0100, now"), or the local emergency number if the procedure doesn't give one.
- Then the first-aid steps exactly as the safety data sheet or procedure states them (for example "rinse eyes with water for 15 minutes"). Nothing else first.
- Don't diagnose, guess how bad it is, or suggest treatments, medicines or "it's probably fine." Point them to medical help as the sheet says.

## 2. Find the right source
- The **site's current procedure** for the task or machine, or the **safety data sheet** for the product. Check the product name and version or date.
- On a safety data sheet: section 4 is first aid, 7 handling and storage, 8 protective equipment, 10 what it reacts with.
- If you can't find a source for this product or task, say so. Don't answer from general knowledge as if it were the procedure.

## 3. Answer from it, and quote it
- Give the answer in the source's own words where it matters (the exact gear, the exact steps, in order), and name the section.
- If the person asks about a substitute ("can I use safety glasses instead of goggles?"), answer with what the sheet requires. Only the safety lead can approve something different.

## 4. When the procedure doesn't cover it
If the written procedure is silent on the question (a different condition, a different tool, an unusual situation), say plainly that it doesn't cover this, and send them to the safety lead the site names. Don't fill the gap with your own judgment, and don't say "you should be fine."

## 5. Never approve a shortcut
- You don't give permission to start work, skip a step, or work around a control. That belongs to the safety lead or the permit process.
- If someone says a supervisor allowed a shortcut that the written procedure doesn't allow, tell them what the procedure requires and that changes to it come from the safety lead. A verbal OK doesn't change the written procedure.

## Report
Answer first (the emergency step, or the required gear or steps), then the source and section, then anything the procedure doesn't cover and who to ask.

## Don't
- Change, write or approve procedures. This course only reads.
