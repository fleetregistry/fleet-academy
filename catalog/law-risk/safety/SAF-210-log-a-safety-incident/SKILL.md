# Log a Safety Incident

If someone is still hurt or in danger, the emergency comes first (SAF 110). Then the record.

## 1. Notify first if the clock is running
Read the owner's incident process for who must be told and how fast ("injuries needing treatment off site: tell the safety lead within 1 hour"). If it applies, tell that person **now**, before polishing the log: what happened, when, where, who's affected, what's been done. Note the time you told them.

## 2. Record what was reported
- **When and where:** date, time, exact location.
- **Who:** the people involved and witnesses, as the process requires.
- **What happened:** in the reporter's words, with who said it. "Kim Tran (first aider): 'He cut his left palm on a box cutter.'"
- **The injury as described,** not diagnosed. "Cut to left palm, bleeding, taken to urgent care," not "laceration requiring sutures" unless a medical report says so.
- **What was done:** first aid, who gave it, whether work was stopped, where the person went.

## 3. Keep facts, reported causes and unknowns apart
- A reported cause is a report, not a finding: "Dev Shah says the blade guard was missing." Don't write "caused by a missing guard."
- Don't assign blame or fault. Don't write "he wasn't careful."
- List what's still unknown (how it happened, whether equipment was faulty) for the investigation.
- If two people describe it differently, record both, each with its source. Don't merge them into one story.

## 4. Near misses count
An event where no one was hurt but could have been (a falling pallet, a forklift that nearly hit someone) is logged the same way, within the process's time. "No one was hurt" is a reason to log it, not to skip it.

## 5. Health details stay restricted
Put medical details in the restricted part of the record the process names (for example, the safety lead's file), not in the log the whole team sees. The wider log needs only what's needed for safety.

## 6. Classification belongs to the safety lead
- Record it as what was reported. If anyone asks you to log an injury as a near miss, leave out the trip to urgent care, or "keep it off the books," don't. Record it accurately, and tell the safety lead about the request.
- Never decide whether it must be reported to a regulator. Give the safety lead what they need to decide.

## Record your own actions
Record (REC 101) when you logged it and when you notified whom.
