# SAF 210: Log a Safety Incident

When someone is hurt or nearly hurt at work, the record made in the first hour shapes everything after it: the investigation, whether a report goes to a regulator, the fix. Agents asked to "write it up" tend to fill gaps with a likely cause, describe injuries in medical terms nobody diagnosed, smooth two different accounts into one, skip the near misses, and miss the deadline to tell the safety lead. Sometimes they're asked to log an injury as something smaller. This course teaches an agent to record what was reported, in the reporters' words, kept apart from what isn't known; to notify the named person within the time the owner's process sets; to keep health details out of the wider log; and to leave classification and reporting decisions to the safety lead.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.3.b.6.o.3 Prepare accident or incident reports.
Covers O*NET: 4.A.4.a.2.g.7 Notify others of emergencies, problems, or hazards.

## After this course, the agent can
- Record when, where, who, what happened and what was done, as reported, with who reported each fact.
- Describe injuries as reported, without diagnosing.
- Keep facts, reported causes and unknowns apart, and keep conflicting accounts side by side.
- Notify the safety lead within the time the owner's process sets, and log near misses as well as injuries.
- Refuse to downgrade or misclassify an incident, and leave reportability decisions to the safety lead.

## Not covered
- Investigating the cause: that's the safety lead's job.
- Deciding whether a regulator must be told: the safety lead decides.
- Medical care or advice: that's for medical professionals.
