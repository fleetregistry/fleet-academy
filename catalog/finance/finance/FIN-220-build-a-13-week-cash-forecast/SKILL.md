# Build a 13-Week Cash Forecast

## Start with facts
1. **Opening cash:** the actual bank balance on a stated date, for example $84,000 on Monday, April 6, 2026. If you can only get last month's balance, say so.
2. **Cash in:**
   - customer invoices outstanding, with due dates,
   - expected new billings,
   - any other receipts (loans, refunds).
3. **Cash out:**
   - payroll dates and amounts,
   - rent,
   - vendor bills due,
   - subscriptions,
   - loan payments,
   - estimated tax payments.

## Build the weeks
- 13 columns, one per week, each starting on the same weekday.
- Rows: cash in by type, cash out by type, net for the week, ending balance.
- Each week's ending balance = the prior ending balance + cash in - cash out.

## Be honest about timing
- Customers often pay late. Use each customer's actual payment history if you have it ("pays about 12 days late") instead of the due date.
- Mark every line **known** (contracted, scheduled) or **estimate**. Write the assumption behind each estimate in a notes column.
- Don't count a sale that isn't agreed yet unless the owner asks you to show a "what if."

## Find what matters
- **The low point:** the lowest ending balance and its week.
- **The minimum:** any week below the owner's minimum cash. If none is set, flag any week below two weeks of payroll and say that's the line you used.
- **The drivers:** the two or three items that move the result most.

## Report
Lead with the answer, bad news first: "Cash falls below your $20,000 minimum in the week of June 15: the low point is $11,200, because quarterly taxes and payroll land the same week. It stays positive all 13 weeks." Then the table, then the assumptions.

## When to stop and ask
- You can't get a current bank balance.
- The forecast shows cash going negative. Report it right away; don't wait to finish polishing.
- Someone asks you to move money or delay payments based on the forecast. That's the owner's decision.
