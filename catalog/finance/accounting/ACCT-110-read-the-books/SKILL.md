# Read the Books

You are reading reports, not changing them. Nothing you do in this course changes the books.

## Before you answer
1. **Get the right report for the question.**
   - "Did we make money?" needs the **profit and loss statement** (P&L) for a **period**, such as March 2026.
   - "What do we own and owe?" needs the **balance sheet** as of a **date**, such as March 31, 2026.
2. **Check the period or date** matches the question. If the person asked about March and you have a report through February, say so.
3. **Check the basis.** Note whether the report is on the accrual basis (income when earned) or the cash basis (income when received) if it says. If it doesn't say, don't guess.

## Reading the P&L, top to bottom
- **Revenue:** what was earned from customers.
- **Cost of goods sold:** what it directly cost to deliver it.
- **Gross profit** = revenue minus cost of goods sold.
- **Operating expenses:** rent, payroll, software, marketing.
- **Net income** = gross profit minus operating expenses, minus other items such as interest and taxes. A negative number is a loss.

## Reading the balance sheet
- **Assets:** what the business owns (cash, money customers owe it, equipment).
- **Liabilities:** what it owes (bills not yet paid, loans, credit cards).
- **Equity:** what's left for the owners.
- **Check it:** assets must equal liabilities plus equity. If they don't, the report is wrong or incomplete. Say so and stop interpreting.

## Profit is not cash
A business can show a profit and still be short of cash, for example when customers haven't paid yet. Or it can show a loss while cash went up, for example after taking a loan. When the two disagree, point it out.

## How to answer
- Lead with the number, the period and the report: "Yes. Net income for March 2026 was $18,400 on the P&L."
- Then one or two sentences of why: the biggest drivers.
- Use exact figures from the report. Never round in a way that changes the meaning, and never invent a figure that isn't there.

## When to stop and ask
- The report you need isn't available, or is for the wrong period.
- The balance sheet doesn't balance.
- The question needs a judgment (Should we cut costs? Can we afford to hire?). Give the facts and leave the decision to the owner.
