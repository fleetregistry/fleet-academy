# Categorize Transactions

You propose. You don't post. Nothing you do in this course changes the books.

## What you need
- The list of uncategorized transactions: date, payee, amount, and the memo if there is one.
- The company's **chart of accounts** (the list of accounts it actually uses). Only propose accounts from that list. If there isn't one, stop and ask for it.
- Any rules the owner has given ("All Delta charges are Travel").

## For each transaction
1. **Read the payee and memo.** "SQ *BLUE BOTTLE" is a coffee shop; "AWS" is cloud hosting; "GUSTO" is usually payroll.
2. **Apply the owner's rules first.** They beat your judgment.
3. **Pick one account** from the chart of accounts. If no account fits, or the item goes on the Needs a person list below, leave the account blank and say why. Don't force a wrong account.
4. **Give a one-line reason:** "Monthly charge from the hosting provider."
5. **Give a confidence level:**
   - **high:** clear payee and an obvious account,
   - **medium:** likely but could be another account,
   - **low:** a guess. Low means it goes to the "needs a person" list.

## Always send these to a person, whatever your confidence
- **Transfers between the company's own accounts** (they aren't income or expense).
- **Owner draws or contributions.**
- **Anything that looks personal** on a business card.
- **Refunds and reversals** (match them to the original charge if you can).
- **Any transaction over the owner's review limit.** If no limit is set, use $2,500 and say you did.

## Hand back
A table with: date, payee, amount, proposed account, reason, confidence. Then a separate short list, **Needs a person**, with why each one is there. Items on that list get no proposed account unless one clearly fits.

## When to stop and ask
- There's no chart of accounts.
- A large share of the list (more than about a quarter) is low confidence. Ask whether there are rules you're missing before guessing.
- Someone asks you to post the categories. Don't. Say that posting changes the books and is outside this job.
