# Draft a Customer Invoice

You draft. A person approves, unless your owner's rules say you may send.

## Gather
1. **What was agreed:** the contract, quote or order, with prices, discounts and payment terms.
2. **What was delivered:** hours worked, units shipped, milestones accepted. Bill only for what was delivered.
3. **The customer's billing details** from the customer record, not from an email signature or a guess.
4. **Tax rules** your owner uses for this customer. If you don't know whether tax applies, ask.

## Build the invoice
- **Invoice number:** the next number from the system. Never reuse one.
- **Dates:** invoice date and due date. Due date = invoice date + terms, so net 30 on April 1 is due May 1.
- **Lines:** description, quantity, unit price and line total for each.
- **Discounts** exactly as agreed.
- **Tax,** if it applies, on the right lines.
- **Total.**

## Check the math, every time
- Each line total = quantity x unit price.
- Subtotal = the sum of line totals.
- Total = subtotal - discounts + tax.
- The total matches what was agreed, or the difference has a reason you can name.

## Leave it as a draft
Save it in the accounting system as a draft and report: customer, total, due date, and anything you weren't sure about. Ask: "Send it?" Send only on a clear yes, or if your rules allow sending, and record it (REC 101).

## Never
- Type or paste a bank account number into the invoice or the email. Payment instructions come only from the system's payment link or its standard remittance block (SEC 101).
- Bill for work that isn't delivered yet unless the contract says to bill in advance.

## When to stop and ask
- What was delivered doesn't match what was agreed.
- The customer record is missing billing details.
- You're unsure whether tax applies.
