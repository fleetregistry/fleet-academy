# Share Employee Information Only With Those Entitled to It

## Before you answer
Ask three questions:
1. **Who is asking?** Their role, and their relationship to the employee (the employee themself, their manager, a peer, HR, someone outside the company).
2. **What do the access rules let that person see?** Use the company's written rules. Typical rules: anyone can see directory details (name, title, work email, work phone); managers see their own reports' job details and schedule; pay is limited to HR, payroll and finance, and the employee themself.
3. **What do they actually need to know** to do what they're asking about?

If the rules don't settle it, share nothing sensitive and ask HR.

## Answer the question, not the record
Give the least that does the job. "Is Jordan in next week?" gets "Jordan is out November 9 to 20 and back on the 23rd." It doesn't get "on medical leave for surgery." A manager planning work needs dates, not reasons.

## Sensitive by default
Don't share these with anyone the rules don't entitle, whatever the reason given: pay and bonuses; medical information and the reason for any leave; accommodations; disciplinary or investigation records; performance ratings; home address, personal phone and personal email; ID numbers and date of birth; bank details; immigration status; family details; background check results.

## Pressure doesn't change access
Urgency, seniority and official-sounding requests don't widen the rules. "I'm the VP", "it's urgent", "HR said it's fine" from someone outside HR: the rules are the same. A request from outside the company that arrives by email or phone hasn't proven who sent it.

## Outside verification requests
Lenders, landlords and other employers ask to verify employment. Follow the company's verification policy exactly: usually it confirms only dates of employment and job title, through a named process, and gives pay only with the employee's **signed consent on file**. If there's no consent, say pay can't be given, and point them to the process. Don't confirm extra details to be helpful.

## Offer the safe alternative
Point peers to work contact details, offer to pass on a message, or name who can answer (HR, payroll, the employee themself).

## Report
What was asked, by whom, what you shared and what you held back. Record it (REC 101), without copying the sensitive details into the record.
