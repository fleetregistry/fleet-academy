# Take In an Employee Concern

## If someone may be in danger, start there
If the employee describes a threat, violence, or a risk of harm now or soon, tell them first how to get immediate help, using what the company's policy names (building security, the emergency number, emergency services). Then route the concern as below, marked urgent.

## Write it down in their words
Record, as the employee told it:
- what happened, in their words (quote the key lines);
- when and where, each time;
- who was involved, and who saw or heard it;
- what they'd like to happen, if they said.
Don't add labels or conclusions ("this is harassment", "sounds like a misunderstanding") unless they're the employee's own words. Don't soften or sharpen what they said.

## Route it to the right person
Send it to the case owner the policy names (often an HR lead or an ethics line). **Never** to the person the concern is about, and not to anyone in their reporting line unless policy says so. Don't copy anyone else "for awareness".

## Be honest with the employee
- **Confidentiality:** say what the policy says, usually that it will be shared only with the people who need to know to look into it. Don't promise that nobody will ever know, or that the person named won't find out. You can't keep that promise.
- **If they ask you not to report it:** if policy requires the concern to be passed to HR (many do for harassment, discrimination or safety), tell them so plainly, before or as you pass it on. Never agree to keep it secret and then report it, or keep it secret when policy says report.
- **Next steps:** who will contact them and by when, from the policy.
- **Retaliation:** if the policy forbids retaliation, say so.
- Don't promise an outcome.

## Don't investigate
Don't contact witnesses, ask the person named for their side, look through their messages, or decide who's right. Even well-meant questions can harm an investigation and the people in it. That's for the case owner.

## Report
To the case owner: the account, the dates, the people named, any safety step taken. To the employee: what you've done, how it will be shared, and what happens next. Record that a concern was taken in and routed (REC 101), without copying the details anywhere else.
