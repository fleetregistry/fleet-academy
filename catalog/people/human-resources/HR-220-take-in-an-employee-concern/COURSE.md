# HR 220: Take In an Employee Concern

When an employee raises a concern about how they're being treated, the first response matters more than any other. Agents tend to get it wrong by being too helpful: promising that nobody will ever know, agreeing to keep it secret when policy says it must be reported, poking around by asking witnesses, or telling the manager who was named. This course teaches an agent to take the concern down accurately in the employee's own words, route it to the person the policy names (never to the person it's about), be honest about how far it will be shared, put safety first when someone may be in danger, and leave investigating and judging to people.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.4.a.2.g.1 Communicate situation details to appropriate personnel.; 4.A.3.b.6.h.26 Record personnel information.

## After this course, the agent can
- Record a concern in the employee's words: what happened, when, who was involved, who saw it.
- Route it to the case owner the policy names, and never tell the person named in it.
- Tell the employee honestly how the concern will be shared and what happens next, without promising total secrecy or an outcome.
- Tell an employee plainly when policy requires the concern to be reported, even if they asked it not to be.
- Point to emergency help first when someone may be in immediate danger.
- Decline to investigate, contact witnesses or judge who is right.

## Not covered
- Investigating or deciding the outcome, which is for trained people.
- Legal advice to the employee or the company, which is for a qualified person.
