# Payroll Change Safety

Before any pay change is entered, every check must pass. If one doesn't, enter nothing and report.

## 1. Written approval, from the right person, for this change
- The company's rules name who approves pay changes (for example the HR director, or the budget owner above a threshold). The approval must come **from that person**, **in writing** (a signed form, or a written approval in the HR or payroll system).
- It must name **this employee**, **this amount or rate**, and **this effective date**.
- A verbal okay, a forwarded "she said yes", or approval from someone else doesn't count, however senior they are. Ask for the written approval.
- You never decide or negotiate pay. You enter what was approved.

## 2. The entry matches exactly
- Match the **unit**: per year, per month, per pay period or per hour. If the approval says "$64,000 a year" and the system field is monthly salary, enter $5,333.33 and show the working. An annual raise is not a monthly raise.
- Match **one-time vs recurring**. A one-time bonus is not a pay rate change.
- Match the **effective date**. Never move it earlier or later yourself. Back-dating creates retroactive pay nobody approved.
- Check the "from" value: if the current pay on the record isn't what the approval assumed, stop and ask.

## 3. The right pay period
Know the period's **cutoff**. A change after the cutoff waits for the next period unless the person the rules name approves a late change. Retroactive amounts are calculated by the payroll system or payroll staff, not by hand.

## 4. Within your limits
If the rules cap what you may enter (an amount, a number of changes), stay within them, and never split a change to stay under a cap.

## 5. Where pay goes is not yours to change
Bank and direct-deposit details are changed only by payroll after verifying with the employee through a contact already on file (HR 350). Never from a message.

## 6. A second look
Every pay change is checked by a second person before payroll runs, using the payroll preview or register. Send it to them with the approval, the before and after, and your working.

## If a check fails
Enter nothing. Say which check failed, for whom, and what's needed. Record it (REC 101).
