# Change HR Records Safely

Before you change any HR record, every check below must pass. If one fails, change nothing, and report.

## 1. The right person asked, with the right document
Each field has an owner. Follow the company's rules; a typical split:
- **The employee themself**, through self-service or a verified channel: home address, emergency contact, preferred name, personal contact details.
- **An approved form** (signed by the people the rules name): title, department, manager, location, schedule.
- **A person in HR or payroll only, never you under this course:** pay, bonuses, bank or direct-deposit details, tax withholding, benefits elections, and employment status (hired, on leave, terminated).

A request that bundles fields gets split: do the ones you may, and say plainly which you didn't and who handles them. A manager saying something "was agreed" is not the document.

## 2. The request is really from them
- A request through the employee's own login to self-service is from them.
- An email, chat or phone call asking to change **where pay goes** is the classic payroll-diversion scam, and it can come from the employee's real mailbox if it was hacked. Never make that change from a message. Send it to payroll, and say it must be confirmed with the employee through a contact **already on file**, not one in the message.
- Don't repeat new bank details in your messages or records (SEC 101).

## 3. The right record
Match the employee ID and the name. If two people share a name, or the ID and name don't match, stop and ask.

## 4. Only the named fields, with before and after
Read the current values first. Change only what the request covers. Write down each field's old and new value.

## 5. Read it back
After saving, read the record again and confirm it shows the new values and nothing else changed.

## 6. Bulk changes
For a change to many records at once, list every record you'll change and compare it to the approval. Change only the ones the approval covers. Hold any extras, name them, and ask.

## 7. Never erase history
Correct a record with a new, dated entry. Don't delete past entries.

## If a check fails
Change nothing for that request (or that part of it), report what failed and what's needed, and record it (REC 101).
