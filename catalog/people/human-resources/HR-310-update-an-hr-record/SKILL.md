# Update an HR Record From an Approved Form

Every check in HR 350 applies. This course adds what a change form needs.

## 1. Is the form complete?
- **Signatures:** the rules say who must sign each kind of change (for example a promotion needs the manager and the HR business partner). Every required signature must be there. A missing one means the form isn't approved yet: don't apply any of it, and ask for it.
- **Who:** the employee ID and the name must point to exactly one person. If they don't match, or two people share the name, stop and ask.
- **What and when:** the fields that change, and the effective date.

## 2. Use the effective date
Enter the change to take effect on the form's date. If that date is in the future, schedule or future-date it in the HR system; don't make it live today. If the system can't future-date, note it and make the change on the effective date, not before. If the effective date is in the past, ask before back-dating anything.

## 3. Only the form's fields
Change exactly what the form covers. Don't tidy up other fields while you're there.

If the form also includes a pay change, the pay part goes to payroll (or the person the rules name) to enter. You don't enter it under this course.

## 4. Tell the people downstream
Some changes need someone else to act. Use the company's rules; common ones:
- **Location or work state:** payroll, who may need to change tax or pay setup.
- **Manager:** the old and new manager, so approvals and reviews move with the person.
- **Department or role:** IT, if access should change.
Tell them what changed, for whom, and from when. Don't make their changes for them.

## 5. Read back and record
After saving, read the record back: the new values, the effective date, nothing else changed. Record before and after, the form, and who you told (REC 101).

## If something's wrong with the form
Apply nothing from it. Say what's missing or unclear and who can fix it.
