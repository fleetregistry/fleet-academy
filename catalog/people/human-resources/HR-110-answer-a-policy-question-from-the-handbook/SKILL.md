# Answer a Policy Question From the Handbook

## Find the right text
1. Use the **current** handbook. If you find more than one version, use the one in effect now and mention that it changed if the asker quoted an older one.
2. Check for anything that applies to **this employee**: a location or state addendum, a union agreement, a role-specific policy, a part-time rule. An addendum that says it overrides the main policy wins for the people it covers. Name which one you used.
3. Find the section that answers the question.

## Answer from what it says
- Lead with the answer, worked out for the asker's numbers if they gave them ("You have 8 days; 5 carry over, 3 expire on December 31").
- Name the section, and quote the key line if the wording matters.
- Don't add what "most companies" do, what the law "usually" says, or what seems fair. If it isn't in the handbook, it isn't the policy.

## When the handbook doesn't say
Say so plainly: "The handbook doesn't cover that." Then say who decides or who to ask (the HR contact the handbook names). Don't answer yes or no as though it were policy, and don't guess.

## Personal, medical and legal questions
If the answer depends on someone's health, family situation, immigration status, a dispute, or their legal rights:
- give the **process** the handbook describes (who to contact, what form, what deadline);
- say that HR decides eligibility, and route the question there;
- don't tell them whether they're legally entitled to anything, and don't interpret laws;
- don't ask for medical details or diagnoses, and don't repeat any they shared beyond the HR contact who needs them.

## You explain; you don't approve
Don't grant exceptions or approve requests ("sure, take the day"). The handbook says who approves; point to them.

## Report
The answer, the section and any addendum used, and who to contact for anything left open.
