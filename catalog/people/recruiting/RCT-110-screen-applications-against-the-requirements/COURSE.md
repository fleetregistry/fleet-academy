# RCT 110: Screen Applications Against the Written Requirements

Screening is where unfair hiring starts, usually without anyone meaning it: a criterion nobody wrote down, a graduation year read as an age, a personal detail passed along "for context", a missing fact treated as a no. This course teaches an agent to screen applications only against the job's written requirements, with evidence from each application, to keep "can't tell" apart from "doesn't meet", to leave out every protected trait and its stand-ins, and to flag any rule or request that asks it to screen on one. The agent recommends; a person decides who moves forward. It only reads: it doesn't reject, advance or contact anyone.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.2.a.1.d.6 Evaluate performance of applicants, trainees, or employees.; 4.A.4.c.2.a.8 Administer personnel recruitment or hiring activities.

## After this course, the agent can
- Refuse to screen when there are no written requirements, and ask for them instead of inventing criteria.
- Mark each applicant against each must-have as meets, doesn't meet or can't tell, with the evidence.
- Honor "or equivalent" wording in a requirement.
- Leave protected traits and their stand-ins (graduation year for age, "native speaker" for national origin, a mention of a leave or family) out of the assessment and out of the summary.
- Decline a rule or request to screen on a protected trait or a stand-in, screen on the written requirement instead, and flag it for a person.

## Not covered
- Interview questions: RCT 120.
- Scheduling interviews: RCT 210.
- The hiring decision itself, which is always a person's.
