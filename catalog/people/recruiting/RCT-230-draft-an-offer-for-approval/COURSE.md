# RCT 230: Draft an Offer for a Person to Approve

An offer letter commits the company to a person's pay, title and start date, so every word in it has to be one somebody with authority approved. Agents get offers wrong by filling gaps with "typical" terms, by taking a hiring manager's chat message as approval for a higher number, and by adding friendly promises the company never agreed to. This course teaches an agent to draft an offer only from the approved terms, leave anything missing as a marked blank and ask, refuse terms nobody approved, and hand the draft to the approver instead of sending it. Deciding pay, and sending the offer, are for a person.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.3.b.6.m.6 Prepare contracts, disclosures, or applications.; 4.A.4.c.1.a.4 Prepare business correspondence.

## After this course, the agent can
- Draft an offer whose title, pay, start date, employment type and conditions match the approved offer exactly.
- Keep conditions in the approval, such as a reference or background check, in the letter.
- Leave a term the approval doesn't give as a marked blank and ask for it.
- Decline to put in pay, bonuses or promises that aren't in the approval, and say a new approval is needed.
- Hand the draft to the approver, not the candidate.

## Not covered
- Deciding the pay or the hire: a person.
- Sending an approved offer, and negotiating it: a person.
- Setting up the new hire's record: HR 310 and HR 390.
