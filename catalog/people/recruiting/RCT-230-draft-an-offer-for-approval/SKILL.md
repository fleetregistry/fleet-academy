# Draft an Offer for a Person to Approve

## The approval is the source
Find the **approved offer**: the record or form signed off by the person the company's rules name (often HR or a budget owner). Every term in the letter comes from it:
- job title, department and manager;
- pay: amount, and whether it is per year or per hour; pay frequency;
- start date and work location;
- full time or part time, and employment type;
- any bonus, equity or allowance;
- conditions, such as a reference check, background check or work-eligibility check.

Use the company's offer template if there is one. Don't rewrite its legal wording.

## Never fill a gap yourself
If the approval doesn't give a term, don't use what's "typical", what the hiring manager said in passing, or what the candidate asked for. Leave a clear blank in the draft ("[START DATE: not in approval]") and ask the approver.

## Terms nobody approved
If someone asks you to put in a higher salary, a signing bonus, a different title or an earlier start date than the approval says, even the hiring manager, even "I'll get it approved later": **don't.** Draft from the approval, and tell them that the change needs a new approval first. A chat message is not an approval.

## No extra promises
Don't add anything the approval and template don't contain: guaranteed raises, a promotion timeline, "permanent" or "job for life", remote work forever, a bonus "you'll certainly get". Friendly-sounding promises can bind the company. If someone wants one added, leave it out and say it's for HR to decide.

Don't drop a condition that's in the approval (for example "contingent on a satisfactory reference check") because it sounds unwelcoming.

## Check before you hand it over
Compare the draft to the approval term by term, and list them side by side for the approver. Any mismatch is fixed or flagged.

## Hand it to a person
Mark it as a draft and give it to the approver. Don't send it to the candidate, and don't negotiate. Keep personal ID numbers and bank details out of the letter and your messages (SEC 101). Record what you drafted and from which approval (REC 101).
