# Prepare a Structured Interview

## Start from the written requirements
List the job's must-haves from the posting or requisition. Every question tests one of them. If a must-have has no question, add one; if a question tests nothing on the list, cut it. If there are no written requirements, ask for them first.

## Write the questions
- Prefer **behavioral** questions ("Tell me about a time you...") and **situational** ones ("What would you do if..."), about the actual work.
- Two to three questions per must-have is plenty. Note which requirement each one tests.
- Follow-ups probe the answer ("What did you do next?"), never the person's life.

## Write the scoring guide before any interview
For each question, describe what a strong, an acceptable and a weak answer look like, on a simple scale (1 to 4). Interviewers score on their own before they discuss.

## The same for everyone
Every candidate for the role gets the same questions, in the same order, scored the same way. That includes internal candidates and people someone already knows. "We know her, just have a chat" makes her impossible to compare with anyone else.

## Questions you never include
Don't ask, even as small talk: age or birth year, graduation year, marital status, children, pregnancy or family plans, childcare, religion or religious holidays, where someone is "originally" from, native language, citizenship beyond what the company's own form asks, health, disability, medications, sexual orientation, gender identity, or arrest record. Don't ask about **pay history**: many places ban it. Ask what pay the candidate is looking for, if the company wants that.

## Turn the worry into the job question
A banned question usually hides a fair one. Ask the fair one, the same way, of everyone:
- "Do you have young kids?" becomes "This role works every other Saturday. Can you work that schedule?"
- "Where are you from originally?" becomes nothing; work authorization is handled by the company's standard form, as HR runs it.
- "Any health issues?" becomes "Can you perform the duties listed, with or without a reasonable accommodation?"

When a hiring manager asks for an off-limits question, leave it out, give the job-related version, and say why in a sentence. If they insist, it's for HR to decide, not you.

## Report
The kit: each requirement, its questions, the scoring guide. Then any question you changed or left out, and why.
