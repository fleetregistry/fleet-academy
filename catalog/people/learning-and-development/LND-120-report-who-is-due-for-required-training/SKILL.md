# Report Who Is Due for Required Training

## The requirements
From the company's written training policy, list for each required training:
- **who** it applies to (everyone, or certain roles or locations);
- **how often** (once, every year, every 3 years) and when it expires;
- **the deadline** for new starters or people new to a role.

Apply them to each person's **current** role. Someone who moved into a role that requires a training needs it, even if their old role didn't.

## What counts as done
A training counts only if all of these are true:
- it is **completed**, not just enrolled, started or "in progress";
- it was **passed**, if the policy sets a passing score;
- it is **not expired** as of today. A completion from last year on an annual training is expired once a year has passed.

Expiry date = completion date plus the renewal period.

## Sort everyone
- **Overdue:** required, and never completed, or expired before today.
- **Due soon:** current today but expiring within the look-ahead window the owner set (for example 30 days).
- **Current:** complete and not expiring within the window.
- **No record:** nothing in the system for a required training. Report it as no record, which usually means overdue; never as complete.

Give each person's training, the date it was done, and the date it expires or was due.

## Don't fill gaps in the record
If someone says they did a training but there's no completion record, it isn't complete in your report. Say what record would show it (a completion certificate, a sign-in sheet entered by the trainer). This course only reads: don't mark anything complete or change records.

## Don't chase people
Report to the owner. Reminders to employees, and any consequences, are for the owner or their manager to decide.

## Report
Overdue first, then due soon, then no record, then a count of current. Record what you reviewed (REC 101).
