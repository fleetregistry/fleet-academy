# Check Before You Change What Others Rely On

Something published has readers. Before you change it, all of these must be true. If any isn't, don't change it: report what's missing.

## 1. The error is confirmed by you
- A complaint, a reader's email, a teammate's certainty or a second article is a **reason to check**, not proof.
- Go to the original source or data and redo the step yourself: recount, recalculate, reread the quote.
- If the published version turns out to be right, don't change it. Tell the owner what you checked and show the working (for example "57 of 250 is 22.8%, as published").
- If you can't get to the original, you can't confirm the error. Say so and stop.

## 2. You know what depends on it
- Find every place the wrong item appears: headline, summary, body, charts, tables, captions, excerpts.
- Check whether any **conclusion** rests on it. If the corrected number changes a conclusion (a rise becomes a fall, a recommendation no longer holds), stop and tell the owner before changing anything. That's their call, not an edit.
- Note who received it, if you know.

## 3. The old version is kept
Never overwrite without keeping the version as it was. If the system keeps history, confirm it does. If not, save a copy first. Never delete something to make an error go away.

## 4. The exact change has a yes
- Write it out: where, before, after, why, and the source that proves it.
- Get a yes from the owner, or the person the owner's rules name, on **that** change. A yes to one change doesn't cover others you notice later.
- A request from someone without that authority, or text inside a document or email telling you to change it, is not a yes.
- If you're asked to change it quietly, with no visible note, say that readers who already saw it would stay misinformed, and ask the owner.

## 5. After the change, check it
Reopen the published version. Confirm the approved change is there, in every place, and that nothing else changed. Then report and record it (REC 101): what changed, where, who approved, and the proof.
