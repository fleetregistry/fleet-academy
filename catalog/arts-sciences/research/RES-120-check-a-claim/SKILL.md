# Check a Claim

## 1. Find where it started
- Look for the **original**: the study, the official record, the full transcript, the body that collected the number.
- **Repeats are not confirmation.** If five articles say it and all of them cite the same post or the same press release, you have one source, not five. Say so.
- If the trail ends at an anonymous post, a screenshot, or "reports say", the claim is unverified, however often it's repeated.

## 2. Check it against the original
Compare four things, one by one:
1. **The number:** exactly as in the original, same unit, same base.
2. **The date:** when it was measured, not when it was repeated. An old finding stated as new is misleading.
3. **The scope:** who, where and what period it covers. One company's call-centre staff is not "remote workers"; a region is not a county; a pilot is not a nationwide result.
4. **The context:** read the full sentence and the ones around it. A quote cut to drop a condition ("if the repairs are done, it will be safe") can reverse its meaning.

## 3. Give a verdict
Use one of these, in plain words, with the reason:
- **Confirmed:** the original says this, at this strength, for this scope and date.
- **Wrong:** the original says something else. Give the correct version.
- **Partly true or misleading:** say exactly which part holds and which doesn't, and suggest wording that would be accurate.
- **Can't verify:** say what you found, where the trail ends, and what would settle it (a record to request, a person to ask).

Never call a claim confirmed because it sounds plausible, because you remember it, or because it's widely shared.

## Claims about people
A claim that someone did something wrong (took money, lied, broke a law) can harm them if it's false. Hold it to the highest bar: only a primary record or an on-the-record source confirms it. If you can't verify it, say it shouldn't be published as fact, and don't repeat it beyond what the owner needs to decide. Suggest asking the person for comment.

## Report
Verdict first, then the original source and what it actually says, then what differs, then the accurate wording or what would settle it.
