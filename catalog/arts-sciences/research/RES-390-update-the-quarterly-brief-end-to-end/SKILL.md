# Update the Quarterly Brief End to End

Each step is a course you've passed. This course is about doing them in order, with proof, and stopping where the owner decides.

## The path
1. **Start from last time.** Read the last published brief and the owner's rules: who owns the data, who approves changes, corrections and publishing.
2. **Check the data (DATA 110).** Row counts, duplicates, total rows, blanks, mixed units, gaps, for this quarter and last. *Proof: what you checked and what you found, with counts.*
3. **Errors in the data.** Don't fix them yet. Work out the right figures in your analysis, say how you treated each problem, and prepare each fix for the dataset owner's yes (DATA 310, DATA 350).
4. **Check last quarter's brief against the data (RES 350).** If a published figure is wrong, prepare a correction for the owner's yes. Don't change the published brief yet.
5. **Compare fairly (DATA 120).** Compare this quarter with last quarter's **corrected** figures, never the published wrong ones. Rates over counts; like-for-like periods.
6. **Sources (RES 110, RES 120, RES 130).** Every outside claim checked against its original. A state or national figure is not a local one; say whose it is.
7. **Charts (DATA 210)** and any images (DES 110): honest axes, every period, gaps shown, cleared assets only.
8. **Draft (WRT 210, WRT 220)** in the owner's voice, using only checked facts, with placeholders for anything missing.
9. **Bring it to the owner.** The draft, then one list of everything that needs a yes: each data fix (with dry-run counts), each correction to last quarter (before and after), and publishing. Nothing is changed or published until they say yes.
10. **After the yes,** in this order: data fixes with a copy, a dry run and verification (DATA 350); corrections with every instance fixed and a visible dated note, plus a note to readers (RES 310); then publish this quarter's brief. Reopen everything you changed to confirm it shows what was approved.
11. **Close the loop.** Tell readers what's new and what was corrected, report to the owner (COM 101), and record each change (REC 101).

## When to stop and ask
- Before any change to shared data, any correction to a published brief, and publishing: always.
- When a dry run doesn't match what was approved (DATA 350).
- When a correction changes a conclusion, not just a number (RES 350).
- **When anything new arrives after the owner approved the draft** that changes a figure or a claim. A yes covers the draft they saw. Don't publish a figure you now know is wrong, and don't change an approved draft quietly: bring the new fact to the owner first.

## Rules for the whole job
- Every figure in the brief can be traced to a row count or a source.
- If a step fails, go back to it; don't skip ahead.
