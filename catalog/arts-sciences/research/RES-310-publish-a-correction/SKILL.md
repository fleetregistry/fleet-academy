# Publish a Correction

Do RES 350 first: the error is confirmed by you, the old version is kept, and the owner said yes to this change. Then:

## 1. Fix every place it appears
Search the whole piece and anything published from it: headline, summary, body, tables, chart labels and data, captions, pull quotes, the email version, social posts or excerpts you control. A correction that fixes the body but leaves the headline wrong hasn't corrected anything. List every place you changed. Where a copy can't be edited (an email already sent, a post the platform won't let you change), correct it with a follow-up: a reply, a new post or a note to the list. Don't delete the original to fix it.

## 2. Add a visible note
Put a short, dated note where readers will see it (at the top, or next to the corrected part):
> Correction, November 4, 2026: An earlier version of this report said Saturday visits averaged 3,150. The correct figure is 3,510.

- Say **what was wrong** and **what is right.** "This article has been updated" tells readers nothing.
- Keep it plain. No long apology, no blame, no burying it at the bottom.
- Change only what the correction needs. Don't use a correction to rewrite other parts.

## 3. Tell the people who received it
If the piece was sent to a list, a client or a team, and the error could affect what they think or do, send a short note: what was wrong, what's right, where to find the corrected version. Draft it and follow the owner's rules about sending (ETH 101).

## Correction or update?
- **Correction:** the piece was wrong when it was published.
- **Update:** it was right then, and new information has arrived (revised official figures, a later event). Label it "Update, [date]:" and say what's new. Don't rewrite the original as if it always said the new thing, and don't call a correction an update to make it look smaller.

## Never
- Delete a piece, or take it down and repost it, to make an error or its comments disappear. That's the owner's decision, and only theirs.
- Edit silently. If the change touches a fact, it gets a note.
- Publish a correction the owner hasn't approved.

## Report
Say what you changed and where, quote the note, say who was told, and record it (REC 101).
