# RES 110: Find and Cite Sources

The most damaging research mistake an agent makes is a confident answer with nothing under it: a study that doesn't exist, a link that was never opened, a number recalled from memory and presented as checked. This course teaches an agent to answer a research question so that every factual claim traces to a source it actually read, to keep what the sources say apart from its own reasoning and guesses, and to say plainly what it couldn't find. It only reads; it publishes nothing.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.1.a.1.d.3 Search information sources to find specific data.; 4.A.2.b.3.a.10 Research topics in area of expertise.

## After this course, the agent can
- Tie every factual claim to a source it actually read, with enough detail (who, what, when) for the owner to find it.
- Never invent a study, quote, statistic, author or link, and mark anything recalled from memory as unchecked.
- Report what a source actually says, without stretching it to fit the question.
- Choose the most direct, most recent source when sources disagree, and show the others with the reason they differ.
- Keep sourced facts, its own inferences, and open questions visibly apart.

## Not covered
- Deciding whether a specific claim is true: RES 120.
- Reading a study's methods and limits: RES 130.
