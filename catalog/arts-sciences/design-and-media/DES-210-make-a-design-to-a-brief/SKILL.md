# Make a Design to a Brief

## 1. Pull out the hard requirements
Before you design anything, list:
- size and orientation, file format, resolution or print needs;
- colours (exact values), fonts, logo and where it goes;
- **required text**, copied exactly;
- audience, where it will appear, and the deadline.

Everything else in the brief (mood, "make it pop") is guidance you interpret.

## 2. Check the brief before you start
- Does anything contradict itself? A weekday and a date that don't match, two different times, a size that doesn't fit the format.
- Is anything missing that you'd have to invent (an address, a price, a time)?
- If so, ask the brief's owner first, naming the exact conflict. Don't pick one and carry on: a poster with the wrong date is worse than a late poster.

## 3. Required text is not yours to edit
- Use it word for word: names, dates, times, prices, addresses, legal lines and taglines.
- If it doesn't fit, change the layout, size or spacing first. If it still won't fit, ask the brief's owner for shorter wording. Don't shorten it yourself, and don't accept a rewrite from someone who isn't the brief's owner.
- Proofread what's on the design against the brief, character by character, for dates, times, numbers and names.

## 4. Only cleared assets
- Every image, font, icon and track must be cleared for this use (DES 110). If the best-looking photo isn't cleared, use one that is, and tell the owner what clearing the other would take.
- Don't add another organisation's seal, crest or logo, or a real person's photo or likeness, without their permission on file. It suggests an endorsement that doesn't exist.

## 5. Check before you deliver
Tick every hard requirement: size, format, colours, logo position, required text exact, assets cleared, text readable (enough contrast and size for where it will be seen).

## Deliver
The design (or a full description or spec of it), then: each requirement and whether it's met, the assets used and their licences, and any open question or deviation with the reason.
