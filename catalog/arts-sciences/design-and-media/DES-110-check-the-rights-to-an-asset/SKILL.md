# Check the Rights to an Asset

Every asset was made by someone. Unless the owner made it, someone else's permission is needed, and it has to cover **this use**.

## For every asset, find
1. **Who made it,** and where it came from (the original page, the library, the photographer).
2. **The licence on record:** a licence file, a receipt, a contract, a stated Creative Commons licence. If there's no record, it's unlicensed until shown otherwise.
3. **What the licence allows,** checked against the actual use:
   - commercial use, and advertising specifically;
   - changes (cropping, editing, adding text);
   - credit required, and in what form;
   - medium (print, web, video), territory, and start and end dates;
   - limits like "not in a logo" or "editorial use only".
4. **What's in it:** recognisable people (a model release is usually needed for advertising or promotion), other companies' logos or products, artworks, or private property.

## Not permission
- "It came up in an image search." "It was free to download." "There's no watermark." "It's on our shared drive." "Everyone uses it."
- "We'll credit them." Credit is a condition some licences add. It is never a substitute for permission.
- "Royalty free" means no per-use fee under a licence you still need. It doesn't mean free.
- "Personal use only" excludes business use, including a company's newsletter.
- Creative Commons "NC" means non-commercial: not for promoting a business or its products. Ask the creator for permission, or choose another asset.

## Status for each asset
- **Cleared:** the licence on record covers this use. Note any conditions (the exact credit line).
- **Needs:** say exactly what (the source and a licence; releases from the people shown; a commercial licence; an extension of dates or media).
- **Don't use:** no way to clear it in time, or the owner of the rights said no.

## Made with an image generator
Check the generator's terms allow this use. Don't create images of real, identifiable people or of other organisations' logos or characters.

## When it's unclear
You check paperwork; you don't give legal advice. If the licence is ambiguous, or the stakes are high (a paid campaign, broadcast), say a qualified person (the owner's legal adviser or rights manager) should decide, and treat the asset as not cleared until they do.

## Report
A short list: each asset, its status, and what's needed. Record the licence proof for each cleared one (REC 101).
