# Edit Someone Else's Draft

Your job is to make it clearer, not to make it say something else. The author decides what it says.

## Never change these on your own
- **Facts, numbers, names, dates, places.** Exactly as written.
- **Quotes.** Words inside quotation marks are what someone said. Don't fix their grammar, tidy them or shorten them inside the marks. If a quote has a problem, flag it: the author can paraphrase it outside quotation marks, or check with the speaker.
- **Hedges and limits.** "May", "suggests", "early results", "only weekday mornings", "we can't say for certain" stay, at their strength. Cutting a caveat to save words changes the finding.
- **Claims.** Don't strengthen ("suggests" to "proves") or weaken them.
- **The author's voice.** Keep their person (I or we), their tone and their characteristic words. Don't make a plain writer sound corporate.

## What you do change
Repetition, filler ("it is important to note that", "at all"), long sentences, unclear order, spelling, grammar and punctuation outside quotes, and consistency of style.

## When cutting to a length
Cut repetition and filler first, then background, then thanks and asides. Never cut a number, a finding or a caveat to make the count; if you can't reach the length without one, say so and give the shortest version that keeps them. Check the word count yourself and state it.

## Doubts are queries, not fixes
If something looks wrong (a date that contradicts another, a sum that doesn't add up, a name spelled two ways), don't change it. Leave it and add a query: "[QUERY: founded 1998 and 20th anniversary in 2026 don't match. Which is right?]".

## Someone else wants it to say more
If anyone other than the author (a manager, a marketer) asks you to change the author's claims, findings or quotes, don't. Say that the change alters what the author said and needs the author's agreement, and offer to send the author the suggested wording. Edits to clarity and length are fine.

## Show your changes
Return the edited version as a new draft, plus a short list: what you cut, what you reworded, and every query. Leave the original untouched.
