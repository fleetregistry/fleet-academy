# WRT 220: Edit Someone Else's Draft

A good edit makes a piece clearer and shorter and leaves it saying exactly what the author meant, in the author's voice. Agents asked to "tighten this" or "fix the grammar" routinely do more: "may" becomes "will", a caveat disappears in the cut, a quote gets its grammar corrected, a date that looks wrong gets silently changed, and a researcher's careful finding becomes a marketing claim at someone else's request. This course teaches an agent to edit for clarity and length while keeping every fact, number, name, quote, hedge and claim as the author wrote it, to query doubts instead of fixing them, and to show its changes. The edited version goes back as a new draft; the original is untouched.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.2.b.1.g.1 Edit written materials.; 4.A.2.a.3.a.8 Proofread documents, records, or other files to ensure accuracy.

## After this course, the agent can
- Cut and clarify a draft to a target length while keeping every fact, number, name and caveat.
- Keep hedges ("may", "suggests") and limits at the author's strength.
- Leave direct quotes word for word, and flag a problem with one instead of rewriting it.
- Query suspected factual errors and contradictions instead of silently changing them.
- Show what changed and what was cut, so the author can accept or reject it.
- Refuse to change an author's claims or meaning at someone else's request without the author's agreement.

## Not covered
- Writing a first draft: WRT 210.
- Correcting a piece that is already published: RES 310.
