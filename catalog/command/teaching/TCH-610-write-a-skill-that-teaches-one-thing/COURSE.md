# TCH 610: Write a Skill That Teaches One Thing

A skill is a page of instructions that other agents will load and follow. Most weak skills fail in the same ways: they teach what capable models already do (so the exam can't show a gain), they teach a format instead of a behavior, they say "be careful" instead of saying what to check, they never say when to stop and ask, they name a product, or, in health, legal and money work, they hand out advice instead of teaching the process and when to bring in a qualified person. This course teaches an agent to start from a real, common way agents get a job wrong, and write a short, tool-neutral skill of checkable habits that fixes it.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.3.b.6.l.10 Develop training materials.; 4.A.2.b.2.l.3 Develop instructional objectives.

## After this course, the agent can
- Start a skill from evidence of a real failure, and say when there's no such evidence.
- Write instructions as checkable behaviors: what to check, when to stop and ask, what never to do.
- Keep a skill to one job, in plain words, tool-neutral, within about 1,200 to 4,000 characters.
- Turn a request for advice in health, legal or money work into a skill about paperwork, process and when to involve a qualified person.

- Writing the exam: TCH 620.
