# Schedule Maintenance

## 1. First: is it an emergency?
Some reports are not maintenance requests. If a report mentions any of these, don't just create a ticket or book the next free slot:
- a gas smell, smoke, burning smell, sparks or scorching,
- water near electrical equipment, or flooding,
- someone trapped (an elevator, a locked room),
- a fire or life-safety system fault (alarm panel trouble, sprinkler leak),
- a structural worry (a ceiling sagging, a crack that's growing).

Contact the owner's on-call person or facilities emergency line **now**, with what was reported, where, and when. Tell the person who reported it to follow the site's emergency procedure (for example, leave the area and call the emergency number or the gas utility). You don't diagnose it or tell anyone it's safe. Then record what you did.

## 2. Is it already open?
Search open work orders for the same asset or location and problem. If one exists, add the new report to it (new details, who reported it, when) and tell the reporter it's already booked. Don't open a second order or send a second vendor.

## 3. Book it to fit the building
- **Disruptive work** (shutting off water, power, heating or cooling, loud work, blocking an entrance or elevator) goes outside occupied hours unless the owner's rules say otherwise.
- **Notice:** tell the occupants affected, as far ahead as the owner's rules say, with what, when and how long.
- **Access:** who lets the vendor in, and to which rooms? Keys, badges, escorts. A vendor who arrives to a locked building is a wasted visit.
- **Approved vendors only,** from the owner's list. A cost over the owner's limit needs a yes first: ask with the quote.

## 4. Inspections and required servicing
Elevators, fire extinguishers, sprinklers, backflow preventers, boilers and similar often have certificates or legal due dates.
- Find the due or expiry date. Book the visit **before** it, allowing for the vendor's lead time.
- If the owner asks for a date after the expiry, say so plainly and suggest the latest safe date.

## 5. The work order
Asset or location, the problem in the reporter's words, priority, vendor, date and time (with time zone), access arrangements, notice sent, cost or quote. Record it (REC 101), and tell the reporter when it's booked.
