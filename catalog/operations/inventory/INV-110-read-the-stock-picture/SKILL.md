# Read the Stock Picture

## 1. Know the four numbers
An inventory record usually shows several quantities. Keep them apart:
- **On hand:** what the system thinks is physically in the building.
- **Allocated (reserved, committed):** on hand, but already promised to open orders. Not free.
- **On hold:** damaged, quarantined, awaiting inspection, expired. Not usable.
- **On order:** bought but not here yet. Not usable today.

**Available = on hand − allocated − on hold.** That is the answer to "can we ship / use this now?" Never answer that question with on hand.

## 2. Get the unit right
Check the unit of measure on every figure: each, case, box, pallet, kilogram, metre. Records often store cases while the question is in units.
- Convert before you compare: 24 cases of 12 = 288 units.
- State the unit in every number you report.

## 3. How long it lasts
If asked when stock runs out, or whether it's enough:
- **Days of cover = available ÷ average use per day.** Count forward from today to get a run-out date.
- Compare that date with the expected arrival of what's on order. If stock runs out before the receipt lands, say how many days the gap is.
- Say the rate you used and where it came from.

## 4. How fresh is the count?
A system figure is only as good as the last physical count plus every transaction since.
- Report the date of the last count when it matters. If it's more than 30 days old, or the item has a history of variances, say the figure may be off and suggest a count before anything important depends on it.

## 5. Answer the question asked
- "Can we ship 120 today?" Yes or no, with the available figure and what's holding the rest.
- "Do we have enough?" Yes or no, and the shortfall in the needed unit.

## Don't change anything
This course only reads. If someone says the real count is different ("the floor lead says it's 500"), don't edit the record, and tell them you can't change it. Report both figures to your owner and say the difference should go through a recount and the stock adjustment process (INV 310, INV 350).
