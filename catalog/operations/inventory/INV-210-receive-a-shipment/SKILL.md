# Receive a Shipment

Three documents meet at the dock: the **purchase order** (what we asked for), the **packing slip** (what the supplier says it sent) and the **count** (what is actually here). The receipt follows the count.

## 1. Find the right order
- Receive against the purchase order the packing slip names, even when an older order for the same item is still open. Leave the older one open, and if it's overdue, tell the buyer it still hasn't come.
- If the slip names no order, or names one that doesn't match the supplier or items, don't guess. If more than one open order fits, ask the buyer which one.

## 2. Put everything in the same unit
Orders, slips and counts often use different units (cases, inner packs, units, pallets). Convert all three to one unit before comparing. 20 cases of 24 and 480 units are the same quantity: that is a match, not a discrepancy.

## 3. Receive what was counted in good condition
- **Received quantity = counted good units.** Never type the order quantity or the slip quantity because it's easier.
- **Damaged:** record separately, with what was wrong ("1 case crushed, contents broken"), and keep it out of available stock (on hold). Photos if the dock has them.
- **Short:** order or slip says more than arrived. Record the shortage. Leave the order **open** for the missing quantity. Don't close it short unless the buyer says so.
- **Over:** more arrived than ordered. Receive up to the order quantity and hold the extra for the buyer to decide.

## 4. Goods that aren't on the order
Items that are on no open purchase order from that supplier don't go into stock and don't get added to an order, even if someone at the dock says "just add them". Put them on hold, record that they arrived, and ask the buyer what to do.

## 5. Tell the buyer
For any short, over, damaged or unordered goods, send the buyer one message: the order number, the supplier, each difference with quantities and unit, and what you did with the goods. This lets them claim from the supplier and stops the bill being paid for goods that never came in good condition.

## 6. Record it
The receipt record shows the order, date, received quantity, damaged and short quantities, and who counted (REC 101). Don't edit the purchase order itself; you create the receipt.

## Stop and ask
- No matching order, or more than one.
- Anything that suggests a safety issue with the goods (leaking, hazardous labels, broken seals on controlled items): leave it alone and tell a person on site.
