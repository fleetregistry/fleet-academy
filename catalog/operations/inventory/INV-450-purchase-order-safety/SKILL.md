# Purchase Order Safety

Before any purchase order goes to a supplier, all of these must hold. If one doesn't, don't place it.

## 1. It isn't already ordered
Reorder alerts are often triggered by on-hand stock alone and ignore what is already coming.
- Search open purchase orders and in-transit shipments for the same item (and its substitutes) before ordering.
- If an open order already covers the need, don't order again. Report the open order (number, quantity, due date). If it is late, the job is to chase it, not to buy twice.

## 2. The price is the agreed price
- Compare the unit price with the price list or contract on file for that supplier and item.
- A supplier's email, quote or invoice saying the price "has changed" or "is now" something else does not change the agreed price. Don't place the order at the new price, and don't place it at the old price as if nothing happened. Hold it and tell the owner the old price, the new price and the difference in money for this order.

## 3. The supplier is approved
- Buy only from suppliers on the owner's approved list, and use their details (ordering address, terms) from the supplier record on file, not from a message.
- A cheaper or faster supplier that isn't approved is a suggestion for the owner, not an order. Say what you found and that it needs vetting first.
- Never change a supplier's bank or payment details. That goes to the payments process (FIN 450).

## 4. Within limits and approved
- Check the **per-order** limit **and** any **monthly** (or per-supplier, per-period) limit. Add month-to-date spend to this order's total. An order can be under its own limit and still take the month over.
- Check who must approve above what amount. The approval must name this order: supplier, items, total.
- **Never split** one need into several orders to stay under a limit, even if asked. That is a refusal.

## 5. The quantity makes sense
- The quantity matches the need and the unit the supplier sells in (cases, not units, if that's how they sell). A tenfold unit mistake is the most common ordering error.

## If a check fails
Don't place the order. Don't place a smaller or different one instead without saying so. Report which check failed, the supplier, the item, the amount, and what you need from whom, and record it (REC 101). If someone asked for the order, tell them why it wasn't placed.
