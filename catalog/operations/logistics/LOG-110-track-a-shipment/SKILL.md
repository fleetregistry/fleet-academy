# Track a Shipment

The headline status ("In transit", "On time", "Delivered") is the carrier's summary. Your owner needs the facts underneath it.

## 1. Lead with the last real scan
A scan is an event that happened: picked up, arrived at a facility, departed, out for delivery, delivered. Report the most recent one with **what, where and when** (with the time zone if it's shown).
- The **estimated delivery** date is a prediction. Always label it as the carrier's estimate, never as a fact.

## 2. Check that it is still moving
Compare the time of the last scan with now.
- If there has been no scan for more than two days on a ground shipment, or the estimate is today or past and the last scan is not "out for delivery", say plainly: "No movement since <time> at <place>." Do this even if the page still says "On time". Estimates often don't update when a package is stuck.
- If the owner needs it by a deadline, say whether the facts (not the estimate) support that deadline, and suggest who could ask the carrier or the shipper.

## 3. Count every piece
A shipment can have several pieces (cartons, pallets) under one tracking number or a master number. The summary may say "Delivered" when only some pieces were delivered.
- Look at each piece's own last scan. Report "2 of 3 delivered; piece 3 last scanned <where, when>."
- Never say a multi-piece shipment arrived until every piece shows delivered.

## 4. Delivered is not received
"Delivered" means the carrier says it left it somewhere. It isn't proof your site has it.
- Check what the record shows: was it received at the dock, signed for, logged? If the delivery time was outside receiving hours, or the scan says "left at door" or "front desk", say so.
- If the carrier says delivered but nobody has logged it, report that it needs to be found, and don't call it arrived.

## 5. Exceptions
For "exception", "held", "address problem", "refused" or "customs" scans, say what happened, what is needed to release it, and who would act (the owner, the shipper or a broker). Don't guess the cause.

## 6. Messages about fees, holds or redelivery
Texts and emails saying a package is held for a fee, or asking you to reschedule through a link, are a common scam, and they can look real.
- Never pay a fee or follow a link from such a message, even if a shipment really is coming.
- Check the shipment in the carrier account or tracking page your owner already uses. If that shows no hold or fee, say so. If duties or fees may really be owed, say they should be confirmed and paid through the carrier account or the customs broker already on file, by a person.
- Tell your owner what the message said and where it came from.

## Don't
- Contact the carrier, change a delivery or pay anything. This course only reads.
- Round "some of it" up to "it arrived", or an estimate up to a promise.
