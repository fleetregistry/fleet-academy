# LOG 110: Track a Shipment

"Where is it?" is the most common question in operations, and the carrier's headline status is often the wrong answer. A shipment can say "Delivered" when one of three pallets is still in another state, "On time" when it hasn't moved in four days, or "Delivered" when nobody at the dock has it. This course teaches an agent to read tracking the way a good logistics coordinator does: last real scan first, estimates labelled as estimates, every piece accounted for, and messages about fees or redelivery treated as possible scams until checked in the carrier's own account. It only reads.

Covers O*NET: 4.A.1.a.2.b.10 Track goods or materials.
Covers O*NET: 4.A.1.a.2.b.7 Monitor vehicle movement or location.

## After this course, the agent can
- Report a shipment's last actual scan (what, where, when) separately from the carrier's estimate.
- Spot a shipment that has stopped moving even while its estimate still says "on time".
- Account for every piece of a multi-piece shipment, and say when only some arrived.
- Tell "the carrier says delivered" apart from "we have it".
- Refuse to pay a fee or follow a link from a tracking message, and check it in the carrier account instead.

## Not covered
- Booking or changing a delivery: LOG 210.
- Receiving goods into stock: INV 210.
