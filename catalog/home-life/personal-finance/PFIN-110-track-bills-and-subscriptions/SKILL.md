# Track Bills and Subscriptions

You read statements and report. You don't cancel, pay, dispute or contact anyone.

## 1. Use enough history
- Read at least three months, and every card and account the owner gives you. A charge seen once is "seen once", not recurring.
- Statement names are cryptic ("SQ *", "PAYPAL *", "DD *"). Match by merchant and amount over time.

## 2. Find the cadence
For each repeating charge, decide how often it comes: weekly, monthly, every few months, yearly.
- **Yearly charges** count once a year. Show the yearly amount and the monthly equivalent (yearly divided by 12). Never add a yearly charge into the monthly total at full price, and don't leave it out. Note when the next one is likely (about a year after the last), so the owner isn't surprised by it.
- A charge that stopped (seen in August, not since) is probably ended. Say so; don't count it.

## 3. Look for the four quiet leaks
- **Price went up:** the same charge is higher than it was. Give the old and new amounts and when it changed.
- **Trial became paid:** a $0 or $1 charge followed by a full-price charge from the same merchant.
- **Likely duplicate:** the same service on two cards or under two family members, or two plans from one provider. Call it a possible duplicate; the owner knows whether both are wanted.
- **Can't identify:** a recurring charge you can't name with confidence. Don't guess what it is and don't call it fraud. List it with the statement name, amount and dates, and ask the owner (or whoever holds that card) what it is.

## 4. The totals
- Monthly total = monthly charges + yearly charges spread over 12.
- Keep unidentified charges visible: show them on their own line, so the owner sees the full amount leaving and what's still unexplained.
- Use the exact amounts from the statements.

## 5. The bills list
For bills (utilities, phone, insurance, loans, cards), keep: biller, last amount, usual range, due date, how it's paid (autopay or by hand, from which account, by its nickname or last four digits only). Never write full card or account numbers.

## Report
1. The monthly total, and what it includes.
2. What changed or needs a look: increases, trials that became paid, possible duplicates, charges nobody can identify.
3. The list, grouped monthly and yearly.

If the owner asks you to cancel or dispute something, say that's the owner's call and action, and give what they need to do it (merchant, amount, dates, which card).

## Don't
- Cancel, pay, dispute or contact a merchant.
- Decide what the owner "doesn't need". Point out what's unused only if the owner told you.
- Copy full card or account numbers anywhere.
