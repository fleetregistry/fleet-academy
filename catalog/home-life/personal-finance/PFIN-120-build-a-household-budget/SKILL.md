# Build a Household Budget from Statements

The point is a true picture. Most budget mistakes come from counting the same money twice or treating unusual months as normal.

## 1. Count each purchase once
Go through every line and ask: is money actually leaving the household here?
- **Credit card payments** from checking are not spending. The purchases on the card are the spending. Count the card's purchases, not the payment, or you'll count everything twice.
- **Transfers between the household's own accounts** (checking to savings, to a kid's account the owner controls) are not spending. Show savings transfers as "saved".
- **Refunds and returns** reduce the category they came from.
- **Reimbursements** (an employer paying back a work expense) cancel the expense; they're not income.
- Loan payments are spending (the whole payment, unless the owner wants principal shown as saving).

## 2. Income: regular and one-time
- Regular income: paychecks and other steady deposits, as they land in the account.
- One-time money (tax refund, bonus, a gift, selling something) goes on its own line. Don't build the monthly budget on it.

## 3. Irregular and yearly bills
Car insurance every six months, property tax, the yearly subscription, school fees: find them in the statements (or ask), and give a **monthly set-aside**: the yearly cost divided by 12. A $750 premium every six months is $1,500 a year, $125 a month. Don't let it land on one month, and don't leave it out because it wasn't in this month.

## 4. Enough data
Use at least three months and average them. With fewer, say so: "Based on one month; groceries may vary." Name any month that was unusual and why.

## 5. The budget
In plain categories (housing, utilities, groceries, eating out, transport, insurance, kids, health, subscriptions, other):
- what came in (regular), what went out, what's left;
- set-asides for irregular bills;
- "Can't tell" for lines you can't place. Ask; don't guess.
Exact amounts, from the statements.

## 6. Choices, not advice
You can show the household's options in its own numbers: "Eating out averaged $410 a month; at $250 you'd keep $160 more." The owner chooses.

**You never give investment advice.** Don't recommend whether or where to invest, specific funds, stocks, accounts or products, or whether to invest versus pay down a loan. If asked, say plainly that it's the owner's decision and, if they want advice, a licensed financial adviser's job, and give the neutral facts they'd bring (balances, loan rates from the statements, the monthly surplus). No tax or legal advice either.

## Don't
- Move, pay or change anything. You only read.
- Copy full account or card numbers into the budget.
