# Change Family Plans Safely

Before you change or remove an existing entry, go through these checks. If one fails, stop and ask.

## 1. Whose is it?
- Change entries the owner created, or ones the owner names exactly ("move Nora's eye appointment").
- An entry someone else created (a spouse, a teenager, a coach's invite, a school calendar) is theirs. If a request would change or remove it ("free up Sunday evening"), don't. List those entries and ask, or suggest the owner check with that person.
- A broad request ("free up the evening", "wipe Sunday") never covers other people's entries or outside appointments. Say exactly which entries you would touch and wait for a yes.

## 2. Which occurrence?
For a repeating entry, decide which the owner means:
- **this one** ("no swim lesson this Monday"): change or cancel only that date;
- **from now on** ("art class moves to 6:30 from the 15th"): change that date and the ones after it; leave earlier ones as they were;
- **all of them**: only when the owner says so.
Never delete a whole series to remove one date. If it isn't clear which, ask.

## 3. Who gets told?
- Cancelling or moving an entry with invited guests can send them a notice. Before doing it, tell the owner who will be notified. If the guests are outside the household, ask first.
- Someone may rely on the entry without being invited: the driver named in it, the carpool parent, the sitter. Say who needs to know, and tell them if the owner has asked you to handle that, or ask the owner who should.

## 4. Did the real thing change?
A calendar entry is a note about an appointment, not the appointment. Moving "Nora - eye doctor" to Monday doesn't move the booking at the eye clinic.
- If the change is about something booked elsewhere (a doctor, a lesson, a class, a reservation), ask whether it has been rebooked or cancelled there.
- Until it has, don't make the calendar say something the office doesn't. If the owner wants the entry moved anyway, mark it "not yet confirmed with the office" and say so.
- Cancelling a lesson or class may have a notice period or a fee. Point it out; that's the owner's decision.

## 5. Before and after
- Read back the change in one line before making it: which entry (title, date, time), what changes, who will be notified.
- Keep the old details (old date and time, guests, notes) in your record so it can be put back.
- After the change, check the calendar shows what you meant, including the occurrences you didn't mean to touch.
- Mark a to-do done instead of deleting it, unless the owner asks for deletion.

## Report and record
Say what changed, what you didn't change and why, who was or still needs to be told, and anything waiting on someone else. Record each change with the old and new details.
