# Compare Travel Options

Compare the trip the family will actually take, not the headline price.

## 1. Total cost for the whole party
For each option, add up what this family will pay:
- the fare or rate times the number of travelers (children often pay full fare);
- **bags**: the bags the family needs, each way. Basic fares may not include a carry-on;
- **seats**: if the family must sit together (young children), the seat-selection fee per person, each way, unless the fare includes seat choice;
- **hotels**: the nightly rate times nights, plus nightly resort or facility fees, parking if they're driving or renting a car, and taxes if the price shown excludes them;
- getting to and from the airport or station, if it differs between options.
Show the sum for each option so the owner can check it. Use the prices given and say when they were seen; prices change.

## 2. Real times
- Times are local to each airport. Note next-day arrivals ("+1") and overnight flights.
- **Connections:** flag any short one, and anything that needs a terminal change, re-checking bags or passing through customs. Domestic connections under about 45 minutes and international ones under about 90 minutes are tight.
- **Separate tickets** (two bookings, often on two airlines) mean that if the first flight is late and you miss the second, the second airline usually owes you nothing: no free rebooking, bags may need collecting and re-checking. Say so plainly whenever an option relies on it.
- A late-night arrival with children, or arriving before a hotel's check-in time, is worth pointing out.

## 3. Change and refund rules
Say for each option whether it can be changed or refunded, at what cost, and until when. A slightly dearer refundable option can be the better buy for a trip that might move.

## 4. Documents
Before recommending anything international, compare each traveler's documents with the rules the owner has or the destination government's own site gives:
- passport validity (many countries require it to be valid a set number of months past arrival or departure; use the stated rule, never a guess);
- visas or entry permits; a consent letter for a child travelling without both parents, if the rule says so.
If a document fails the stated rule, say so first: nothing should be booked until it's fixed. If you can't find the rule, say it must be checked; don't assume.

## 5. Present it
Two or three options, each with the total, the times, the risks and the rules. Then a recommendation tied to what the owner said matters (cost, timing, flexibility, kids' comfort), and what's still unknown.

## Don't
- Book, hold or pay for anything.
- Present a headline fare as the trip's cost.
- Give immigration advice beyond repeating the stated rules.
