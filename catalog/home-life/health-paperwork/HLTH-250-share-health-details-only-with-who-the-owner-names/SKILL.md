# Share Health Details Only With Who the Owner Names

Health details, once shared, can't be taken back. Before anything leaves your hands, run these checks.

## What counts as health details
Diagnoses, conditions, medicines, allergies, test results, the kind of doctor or clinic (therapist, oncologist, behavioral health), appointments with them, insurance claims and denials, and medical bill lines, which reveal the service. A file name or calendar title can carry them too.

## 1. Whose details, and who may see them?
- Each person's health details belong to that person (for young children, to their parents).
- Share only with people the owner has **named** for that person's details, and for the reason they were named: "Grandpa Joe may know the kids' allergies while they stay with him."
- Anyone not named, however close (a grandparent, a friend, a coach, a teacher, another parent): don't share, even a hint ("it went fine", "nothing serious"). Reply kindly that it's not yours to share and that you'll let the owner know they asked. Then tell the owner who asked what.
- Not sure someone is named, or for this? Ask the owner first. Someone saying the owner or the patient said it's fine is not the owner saying it: check with them before sharing.

## 2. Only what the task needs
A named person gets what their job needs and no more. Grandpa Joe, looking after Ava for the weekend, needs her bee-sting allergy, where her emergency medicine is kept and the doctor's written plan for using it, and who to call. He doesn't need her other diagnoses, medicines she won't take while she's with him, or her appointments.

## 3. Don't trust who contacted you
A call, email or text "from the insurer", "from the doctor's office" or "from the pharmacy" asking you to confirm a member ID, date of birth, diagnosis or claim details: **don't confirm or send anything.** Real offices can be reached on the number on the insurance card or the provider's own bill. This holds even when the message comes from the office's usual address or quotes a real claim number: mailboxes are hacked and addresses faked, and a real office doesn't need you to send health details back to a message. Tell the owner what was asked and by whom (point out a sender address or number that isn't the one the office usually uses), and suggest they call that number themselves.

## 4. Forms
School, camp, sports and work forms ask for health information.
- Fill in only what the owner has given you or approved for that form.
- Don't fill health fields from records you happen to have.
- Leave the rest blank and tell the owner which fields are waiting for them.

## 5. Where it goes
- Never put health details on shared calendars, family group chats, shared folders or file names others can open. Use plain words ("Ava - appointment") and keep details where only the owner (and named people) can see them.
- Never send health details to a new address, number or site the owner hasn't named.

## 6. No medical advice
Sharing a doctor's written instructions with a named person is fine. Adding your own medical judgment is not.

## Record
Record what you shared, with whom, and under which of the owner's permissions. Record declined requests too, without repeating the details.
