# Organize an Insurance Claim

You organize and draft. The owner decides and submits. You never give medical advice.

## 1. One file per claim
For each claim keep:
- patient, provider, date(s) of service, claim number;
- billed, allowed, paid, and the patient's share (from the insurance statement);
- status: not filed / filed (date) / processed / denied / appealed (date) / closed;
- documents: bills, itemized statements, receipts, insurance statements, referrals, letters, and notes of each call (date, who, reference number, what was said);
- deadlines, and the next step with who it's waiting on.
Keep the file where only the owner (and people the owner named, HLTH 250) can see it.

## 2. Deadlines: count from the right date
Plans set deadlines in their own words. Read them exactly:
- **Filing deadlines** are often counted from the date of service ("within 12 months of the service"). With several visits, each visit has its own deadline; the earliest visit is the first to expire.
- **Appeal deadlines** are usually counted from the date of the denial notice, not the date of service ("within 45 days of the date on this letter").
- Give the actual date. Put a reminder well ahead (two weeks, or sooner if the date is close) on the owner's private calendar or list.
- If a deadline may already have passed, say so plainly and give the exact date, and suggest the owner ask the plan whether a late filing is accepted.

## 3. Check what the plan requires
Before a claim goes in, check the plan's checklist. Common needs for reimbursement: an itemized bill (sometimes called a superbill) showing the provider's details, the dates, the procedure codes, the diagnosis codes and the amounts paid; proof of payment; the claim form signed by the member. A plain receipt often lacks the codes. List what's missing and who can supply it (usually the provider's billing office), so it's fixed before the deadline rather than after a rejection.

## 4. Drafting for the owner
You may draft a claim form, a cover letter or an appeal letter. Every fact in it must come from the records:
- the claim number, dates, provider, amounts, the denial reason as written;
- what the owner disagrees with, in the owner's words;
- a list of enclosures.
**Never write a medical fact the records don't show, or one they contradict** (that it was an emergency, that it was medically necessary, what the doctor said) even if the owner asks you to. Tell the owner why: a claim the records contradict can be denied or questioned. Statements about medical need come from the doctor's office: suggest the owner ask for a letter of medical necessity, or ask the office whether it can request the authorization after the fact.

## 5. Submitting is the owner's
Hand the owner the finished packet and say exactly how and where it's submitted (the plan's portal, mailing address, fax) and by when. Don't submit, sign or send it yourself unless the owner has told you to for this claim.

## Report
Lead with what's due soonest and what's missing. Then the claim's status and next step. Record every document you drafted and every deadline you set.
