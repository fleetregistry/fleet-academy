# Caring for Houseplants

## Light
1. Learn what light the plant wants before choosing a spot. Most leafy plants like bright light that does not hit the leaves directly.
2. A window facing north gives soft, even light all day. A window facing south gives strong light; set plants a few feet back from it.
3. If the plant leans hard toward the window, turn the pot a quarter turn each week so it grows evenly.
4. Pale, stretched stems usually mean too little light. Brown, crisp patches usually mean too much.

## Water
1. Push a finger into the soil up to the first knuckle. Water only when that depth feels dry.
2. Water slowly until a little runs out of the drainage hole, then empty the saucer after ten minutes.
3. Never let a pot sit in standing water. Roots need air as much as they need water.
4. Plants drink less in winter. Check the soil as usual, but expect to water about half as often.
5. Yellow leaves with soft stems usually mean too much water. Drooping leaves with dry soil mean too little.

## Soil and pots
1. Use a pot with a drainage hole. A decorative pot without one can hold a plain plastic pot inside it.
2. Choose a potting mix made for indoor plants. Garden soil packs down and drowns roots.
3. Move a plant to a pot one size larger when roots circle the bottom or poke out of the hole. Spring is the best time.
4. After repotting, water well and keep the plant out of strong sun for a week.

## Feeding
1. Feed only while the plant is growing, usually spring and summer.
2. Use a liquid plant food at half the strength the label suggests, about once a month.
3. Skip feeding a plant that is sick, newly repotted or resting for the winter.

## Cleaning and checking
1. Wipe dust off broad leaves with a damp cloth every few weeks so they can take in light.
2. Look under the leaves when you water. Tiny webs, sticky spots or cotton-like patches mean pests.
3. Move a plant with pests away from the others, rinse it in the shower, and wipe the leaves with mild soapy water.
4. Snip off dead or yellow leaves at the base with clean scissors.

## Humidity and temperature
1. Most houseplants like the same temperatures people do, and dislike cold drafts and hot air from vents.
2. In dry winter air, group plants together or set them on a tray of pebbles with a little water below the pots.
3. Keep plants off cold windowsills on winter nights.

## When to start over
1. If most of the roots are brown and mushy, cut a healthy stem and root it in a glass of water.
2. Change the water every few days. When the new roots are a couple of inches long, plant the cutting in fresh soil.
